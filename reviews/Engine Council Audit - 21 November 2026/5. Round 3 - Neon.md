## 1. Score

**7.5 out of 10.**
**Is it a 9 or better? No.**

The foundation is strong and most of the hard architectural calls are right: one repository, one Remotion host with thin film folders, immutable `EngineContext` with safe-box-relative limits, pure-type taste defaults baked in, viewer-true browser inspection via `kte sheet` and draft audio renders, and sensible rulings in Section 9. None of the settled rulings in items 152 to 169 need to be reopened.

It is not a 9 as written because five concrete design holes sit right in the middle of what Jefferson cares about most (Pulp Fiction storytelling transitions, fewer revision rounds, and fast turnaround on *Room for One*). If someone built this document verbatim tomorrow, they would hit blocking ambiguities in the compiler, the effect runtime contract, and two hard-error gates that will false-fire on real songs.

## 2. Specific changes to reach 9 out of 10

1. **Specify the spatial placement and portrait-reflow contract in `compile/` (Section 2, items 21 to 29; Section 8, item 135).**
   Right now a line card names `from` (where it grows from) and `enter` (how it moves), but never defines where the line *rests* on the 2D board or how `size: 'hero' | 'second'` maps to cap heights. Step 7 reduces the entire compiler to a six-phrase mapping table. Add:
   - An optional `place` field (such as `below`, `right`, `left`, `inside`, `overlay`, `turnCW`, `turnCCW`, defaulting to `below`) that tells the compiler how to compute the incoming block's resting `(x, y, rotation)` from `from`.
   - Named size tiers (`hero`, `body`, `second`) mapped to fractions of `ctx.safeBox`.
   - A clear board-space vs camera-space rule for `quarterRoll`: placing the incoming line 90 degrees rotated on the board and rolling the camera to read it upright naturally turns the previous line into a margin note, without double-rotating the old line.
   - The `rows: 'auto'` portrait reflow contract: wrap rows to fit `ctx.safeBox.width` at or above the format cap floor, recompute relative placement from `from`, and emit a named compile error if a block cannot fit rather than silently shrinking.

2. **Fix `EffectDef` and define how `engine/` evaluates new effects without breaking the dependency rule (Section 1, items 10, 15; Section 8, item 134).**
   Item 134 forces every `EffectKind` into `pose(u, unit, p, env)` and `ink(u, unit, p, env)`, which does not fit `camera` recipes (they emit `RigKey[]`, not poses or ink) or paired `transform` effects like `matchCut` and `slotRoll` (which must pin or suppress kept substrings on the *outgoing* block while `old` animates the remaining glyphs away). Split `EffectDef` into three clean shapes: `UnitEffectDef` (`enter`, `exit`, `accent`), `PairTransformDef` (`transform`, receiving outgoing and incoming blocks and returning an optional kept-glyph exclusion mask for `old`), and `CameraRecipeDef` (`camera`, returning `RigKey[]`).
   In addition, resolve the Item 10 / Item 15 gap: `engine/` cannot import `effects/`, so `compileScore` must resolve all used effects (including `cascade`, `reverse`, and `film:` local effects) into a read-only `EffectTable` attached to `CompiledFilm` (or `ctx`) that `wordStateAt` reads, instead of relying on hidden global registration. State explicitly how `wordStateAt` returns per-glyph mods so `cascade` by glyph/syllable has a real runtime target.

3. **Define reply lifecycles, motif id scoping, and cross-scene `matchCut` rules (Section 2, items 21 to 24, 28).**
   The worked example relies on three behaviors that the prose never defines:
   - **Reply lifecycle**: state that `old` applies to the whole previous line (call and reply) unless the incoming line or its reply references a landmark inside the previous reply (as `h.3` references `h.2.reply` and `h.4.reply` references `h.3.reply`), in which case that reply stays alive until the referencing effect's `release` frame.
   - **Cross-scene handoffs**: in `verse1.ts`, `v1.1` starts a new page/scene yet uses `from: word('h1.4.reply', 'ONE')` with `matchCut({pin: 'screen'})`. Allow a scene opener to reference a landmark from the prior scene's final frame specifically for screen-space coordinate handoff, computed from the kept substring's rendered screen box at transfer.
   - **Motif id rewriting**: state in item 24 that inside `motif('h', ...)`, landmarks prefixed with `h.` are rewritten per occurrence (`h1.`, `h2.`, `h3.`), while all other ids (`count.2`) resolve as external ids.

4. **Fix two hard-error rules that will false-fire on real songs (Section 2, item 29; Section 6, items 117.1, 118.2).**
   - **Item 29 (old line still singing)**: in fast hip-hop and call-and-response tracks like *Room for One*, consecutive lines often have fewer than 14 frames between the previous last token and the next onset, so `prevLastTokenEnd > nextEntranceStart` will happen constantly. Change item 29 so `stay()` and `dock()` pass whenever the old line stays legible through `prevLastTokenEnd`; for `clear(exit)` and `retract()`, delay exit start to `max(nextEntranceStart, prevLastTokenEnd)` within a small tolerance, and fail only when the incoming line physically hides or collides with a word that is still being sung.
   - **Item 117.1 (ink outside the frame or safe box)**: scope this hard master error to **reading words during their rest/reading interval**. As written, any `edge('B')` entrance, `fall()` exit, or offscreen slide blocks a master. Also state clearly in item 118.2 that the conservative node hull pass flags frames for `kte sheet` review and does not hard-block masters on its own conservative over-estimates.

5. **Close the ear-check persistence loop and `kte/*` type resolution (Section 5, items 109, 111, 114.1; Section 6, items 117.1, 119).**
   - Item 117.1 blocks masters on unresolved ear-check items, but item 119 only outputs markdown (`analysis/Ear check list.md`), which `kte ingest` would overwrite. Store manual timing overrides and verified ear-check resolutions in a structured author-owned file (either `analysis/ear-check.json` or a section inside `song.json`). `kte ingest` reads it, preserves verified entries, and regenerates the markdown as a human view; `kte check` gates masters from the structured file.
   - State how `'kte/score'` and `'kte/effects'` resolve at runtime (esbuild alias injected by `host`/`cli`) and during editing/typechecking (either `kte check` injects a virtual `tsconfig` mapping `kte/*` to the pinned release, or `kte new` writes a minimal generated `tsconfig.json` while still excluding `package.json` and `node_modules`).

6. **Close the Step 8 bootstrap gap and restore the missing Step 3 investigation (Section 2, item 30; Section 8, items 131, 136).**
   - Scope item 30 so only required fields (`from` on non-openers, `enter`, and `old` when neither a scene opener nor a `dismissesOld` effect) fail a master when omitted. Define the concrete compiler fallback in draft mode (for example `from: prevRow`, `enter: rise()`, `old: stay()`) so Step 8's zero-hand-edit `kte new` to `kte render --draft --section h1` actually runs before `kte draft` is built in Step 9.
   - Add the smear-ordering and per-letter `textLength` investigation promised in item 168 into Step 3 (item 131) so it does not fall through the cracks.

7. **Rebalance Steps 6, 8, and 10 (Section 8, items 130, 134, 136, 138, 140).**
   Make the three build-cost adjustments detailed in Section 4 below so *Room for One* starts faster on the real score format *with* its signature storytelling effects ready, instead of falling back to legacy hand-built boards.

## 3. Errors and contradictions in the document

Beyond Fable's two flagged slips in the worked example (missing `row` and `edge` imports; motif ids `h.1` vs `h1.4.reply`):

1. **Wrong module imports and split ownership in the worked example (Section 2 vs Sections 1, 3, 8).** `score/hook.ts` imports `cascade` and `retract` from `'kte/score'`, but Sections 1, 3 (items 33, 80), and 8 (item 134) place both in `effects/` (`'kte/effects'`). `stay` and `dock` are defined as score fates in item 22.4 yet also listed in the Section 3 effects table (items 87, 88). `hook.ts` also imports unused `stay`, `type`, and `fall` (`fall` is used in `verse1.ts`, not `hook.ts`).
2. **`old: clear(retract())` contradicts item 22.4.** Item 22.4 lists `retract()` as a top-level fate alongside `clear(exitEffect)`, yet line `h.2` writes `old: clear(retract())`. Pick one form.
3. **`slotRoll` omits `old` without declaring `dismissesOld`.** Line `h.4` omits `old` with `enter: slotRoll(...)`, which item 22.4 allows only when the effect declares `dismissesOld`. Item 68 (`slotRoll`) does not declare `dismissesOld` (only items 63 and 64 do). Similarly, item 22.4 forgets to exempt scene openers from `old` (unlike item 22.2 for `from`), and item 30 says "A field left out" blocks a master without excluding optional fields (`reply`, `accents`, `portrait`, `repeat`, or defaulted `rows`/`camera`).
4. **Double rotation in `h.3`.** Line `h.3` specifies both `old: dock('margin')` (commented "line 2 quarter-rolls into a margin note") and `camera: quarterRoll({dir: 'cw'})`. Rolling both the outgoing block and the camera double-rotates the previous line unless the compiler explicitly suppresses one.
5. **Item 15 dependency rules contradict Sections 5, 6, and 8.**
   - `engine/` cannot import `effects/`, leaving no declared path for `wordStateAt` to execute `defineEffect` / `film:` effects.
   - `check` is barred from importing `score`, `song`, `effects`, and `react-dom/server`, yet items 116.1 (score check), 116.4 (sync evidence), 116.6 (variety / `repeat: 'intended'`), and 121 (golden SVG via `react-dom/server`) require them.
   - `host` is barred from importing `score` and `song`, yet it loads and compiles the film's score and song map.
6. **Step 8 acceptance test contradicts item 109 and Step 9.** Item 136 requires `kte new` followed immediately by `kte render --draft --section h1` with zero hand edits, yet item 109 says `kte new` emits unfilled fields, `kte draft` is not built until Step 9 (item 137), and no draft compiler fallback is defined.
7. **Item 168 is missing from Step 3.** Item 168 explicitly puts Neon's smear-ordering claim into Step 3 as an investigation item, but item 131 lists six exact commits and omits it.
8. **Item 117.1 contradicts `edge()` entrances and offscreen exits.** Making any "ink outside the frame or the safe box" a hard master error blocks `edge('B')` (used in hook occurrence 2) and offscreen exits (`fall`, `fling`, `shove`), and conflicts with item 118.2 calling the hull pass conservative and non-passing.

## 4. Build cost vs benefit (and item 142)

Fable is right to flag the eight-step pre-film runway in item 142. However, **falling back to legacy hand-built boards after Step 5 is the wrong trade**: Jefferson would pay another full video of manual board assembly and revision rounds while delaying real validation of the score compiler and portrait pipeline by an entire production cycle.

The better fix is to cut two pieces of front-loaded engineering out of Steps 6 and 8 so *Room for One* starts on the real score format much sooner:

1. **Do not wrap all ~40 existing verbs with bespoke schemas and `ink()` functions in Step 6.** Item 134 demands a typed schema and an `ink()` function for every existing row in Section 3 before Step 7 even starts, even though `ink()` is not consumed until Step 11. Instead, build `EffectDef`, `cascade`, and `reverse` in Step 6, wrap only the ~10 to 12 core verbs used in the fixtures, and give the remaining legacy verbs a single generic passthrough adapter with a default bounding-box `ink()` until Step 11 or when a verb is actually used.
2. **Pull the ~6 signature Pulp Fiction effects into Step 6/7, out of Step 10.** Today, the worked example for *Room for One*'s own hook relies on `matchCut`, `retract`, `dock`, `slotRoll`, `stamp`, and `quarterRoll`, all of which sit in Step 10! Starting *Room for One* after Step 8 without the effects that make the hook work forces throwaway placeholders or `raw()` blocks. Swap the effort: defer the 30-verb legacy wrap and build those 6 high-leverage effects first.
3. **Move the multi-release manager out of Step 8 into Step 12.** Building `kte release install`, `git archive`, `npm ci` release trees, and tag-dispatching in `bin/kte` before `v1.0.0` exists adds real build and debugging cost with zero payoff during the pilot (`Room for One` runs on `"engine": "dev"` until Step 12 anyway). Keep `film.json` carrying `"engine": "dev"` in Step 8, run `bin/kte` directly against the repo, and build release installation and `kte pin` in Step 12 when `v1.0.0` is actually cut.
4. **Time-box Step 2's SSR spike.** Because `react-dom/server` on `TypeStage` is unverified (item 3.1), cap the proof at 30 minutes. If `TypeStage` does not cleanly render in node, fall back immediately to hashing the serialized `wordStateAt` output across the 12 fixture frames rather than stalling Steps 3 to 7.

With those seven fixes and the Step 6/8/10 rebalance, this design cleanly clears 9 out of 10 and delivers Jefferson's goal without burning an extra video on legacy boards.
