## Section 1. Score

6. **5 out of 10 for this goal.** The core is good and worth keeping: word state is a pure function of board and frame shared by the renderer and a node validator, layout comes from measured metrics, and every block must declare what it emerges from. But the engine is authored at the wrong altitude for "crank them out" (hand-placed world coordinates, global word indices and literal frame numbers, about 8,100 lines of boards for one song), its defaults and rule families encode the first video's style bible rather than his standing taste (shadows on, 47 percent of Ball's entrances are `rise`, `two-scales` waived ten times in two films), and the per-video shell, the vertical and the engine's own version state are all hand work.

## Section 3. Architecture findings

### What is right (keep all of it)

19. **One state function for render and check.** `wordStateAt(board, i, f)` (`engine/wordState.ts:241`) is what the renderer draws and what the validator measures, and layout uses the metrics file, so node and Chrome agree. This is the engine's main asset; everything I propose compiles down to it.
20. **Emergence is a required declaration** with an anchor that must resolve (`types.ts:248-262`), which turns "comes out of the previous arrangement" from a hope into a checked fact.
21. **Letter anatomy anchors** (`types.ts:230-243`, `engine/letters.ts`): flank, foot, counter, stroke. This is the Pulp Fiction anchor letter, and nothing off the shelf has it.
22. **The rig**: one camera per board, pure, with framed targets, `about` points and named moves.
23. **The engine is already clean of props and nearly clean of Remotion.** No file under `engine/` imports from `props/`, and the only Remotion import is `useCurrentFrame` in `engine/time.ts:2`. Isolating props is therefore cheap (Section 4, item 62), and drawing a frame without Chrome is plausible (item 58).
24. **The validator is fast enough**: 25 s for a 22 s chunk, so a whole song is a few minutes. It can run on every save.

### What is wrong, ranked by effect on per-video effort and revision rounds

25. **Authoring altitude is the largest cost.** A block is placed in world coordinates with global word indices and song seconds (`types.ts:109-177`), and builders have answered by writing their own layer per chunk: `film/src/drawings/b/stations.ts` defines `Pose`, `poseAt`, `s2w`, `w2s`, `rowAt`, `target`, `panBy` so that "each line is laid out in the screen coordinates of the camera pose it rests at, then turned into world coordinates" (its header), with 640 and 360 hard-coded. Timing is literal frames (`TOAST_OUT = 1944`, `at: fs(1955)` in `b-verse2.ts`). Every one of those numbers is a place where a note from him, a changed alignment or a different format means hand edits. KIT 46.14 named the front end as "optional, recommended" and it was never built; it is the thing to build.
26. **Builders cannot see what they make.** Only the main agent renders (brief 71), so a builder works from validator text, a reviewer who cannot watch video gates it, and the first time anyone sees motion at speed with sound is often when he does. That is a revision round built into the process. See item 58 for a way to give sandboxed builders stills.
27. **The film is built as separate chunk modules joined by hand-declared handoffs.** Handoff pictures are measured screen boxes typed into `song.ts` (Ball's `'H0-A'`, with pixel positions read off a frame); once one exists every module must declare one; En Fuego waives `handoff` four times; `Full.tsx` holds patched copies of boards. Seams are a product of the build method (parallel builders, one module each), not of the film.
28. **The rule set does not fit the style he is asking for.** In Ball and En Fuego together `two-scales` is waived 10 times, `variety` 6, `level-run` 4. Ball switches on both opt-in families at once (`sheetRules` on 12 boards, `transitRules: 'energy'` on 9). Rules from the first video's style bible (giant-level rests, camera angle must change every second line) cost loops that no note of his asked for, while the things his notes did ask for (entrance variety, in sync with the vocal, small type readable) have no rule. Fable's own diagnosis of the scrolling-feed vertical was that the gating pushed builders to `rise` (brief 62), and the counts in item 15 say it still does.
29. **Words are identified by global index.** Every board, word rule and constant set (`HOOK_LINES = new Set([0, 1, 14, ...])` in Ball's `song.ts`) is keyed to positions in one song map. A corrected alignment, a merge, or a different cut shifts them. This is why the Radio Edit vertical could not import the master's boards at all (`vertical/song-radio.ts` rule d) and why hook helpers were copied.
30. **The per-video shell is copied** (brief 52): config, tsconfig paths, Root, eight identical chunk compositions, wrappers, a lock file from another film, metrics, fonts. I confirmed the relative alias in `film/remotion.config.ts` ("a render worktree in /tmp fails") and the wrapper `film/tools/validate.mjs`, whose own comment contradicts the engine tool it wraps (`tools/validate-boards.mjs` resolves against the current directory).
31. **The engine has no version a film can stand on.** Four videos' worth of features (the 3D turn, energy rules, spread, wah, clearing disc) are uncommitted; pointer files name a commit the film does not render from. Any engine edit can silently change a finished film, and there is no test that would notice (no golden frames; the fixtures validate but nothing compares output).
32. **Verb parameters are untyped.** `VerbCue.params` and `Emergence.params` are `Record<string, unknown>` (`types.ts:71`, `:255`) read through `P(c, 'key', default)` (`engine/emergence/common.ts:17`). A misspelt or misplaced parameter silently takes the default. Film verbs are used through casts (`'glide' as EmergeMove`, per En Fuego's verbs header). A builder can pass the validator with a move that is not doing what the storyboard said.

### Ranked by effect on output quality

33. **Defaults contradict his standing taste.** Shadows are on for every E2 and E3 word on a dark ground (`engine/TypeStage.tsx:66`) and under every plate (`:113`, `:141`, `:165`); each film turns them off word by word in a word rule. Parentheses are stripped per film, and stripping them through `opts.text` also froze the case, which the vertical had to work round by stripping in the data instead (`vertical/song-radio.ts`, the note before `rawWords`). Taste that has to be re-applied per video will be missed in some video.
34. **Nothing measures entrance variety**, and the library's use is concentrated (item 15). The `variety` rule checks camera angle only (`engine/validateRules.ts:374`, `:383`).
35. **Exits are second-class.** `Block.exit` supports only `style: 'turn'` (`types.ts:39-46`). Every other way out is a separately shaped verb (`vanish`, `closeAway`, `sinkBehind`, `slideAlong`, `tumble`) or a board exit, and the default fate is to dim to 45 percent and stay (`wordState.ts:86-89`). Pulp Fiction dismisses text as deliberately as it introduces it; here an entrance has twenty named forms and an exit has one plus a handful of special cases. Ball needed 54 `turn` uses partly because turning out is the one exit a block can declare.
36. **Good moves are stranded in films.** Thirteen film-local verbs in Ball, sixteen or so in En Fuego, seventeen in DOGEfather; three were copied by hand between films. The moves he is now asking for by name (slides, bounces, punches, arcs) are exactly the ones that never reached the engine.
37. **Sync has one-sided checks.** `display-late` exists (`validate.ts:248`) and `slide-early` covers one move (`validateMotion.ts:113`), but there is no general readable-before-sung rule (backlog 10), although three films drew notes or rulings on it.
38. **Legibility floors miss part of what is displayed**: letters in motion (item 12), ghost copies, and there is no contrast check of type colour against the ground it is actually over.
39. **No perspective camera.** All rig moves are 2D (position, zoom, roll). The 3D turn is per block. "Turning in from the third dimension" is covered for words, not for the board.

### Ranked by effect on reuse across videos and formats

40. **Configuration is a module singleton** (`config.ts:99-153`): one song and one frame size per bundle, last call wins, so a vertical is a second entry point with five ordering rules (`vertical/song-radio.ts` a to e). Film code also captures live values at import time (the header mentions `hook.ts` building `BEATS` at import), which is what makes the ordering fragile.
41. **Nothing restages.** A vertical is a new set of boards from scratch because a board is absolute coordinates for one frame shape (item 25) and one song map (item 29). Fix those two and restaging mostly falls out.
42. **Caches are keyed on object identity.** I count 12 `WeakMap`s keyed on `Board` or `VerbCue` (`layout.ts:387`, `rig.ts:66`, `wordState.ts:72`, `:102`, `:133`, `:136`, `:194`, and others). A board mutated after first use is silently stale (the Midnight on Midnight bug). The cheap fix is to deep-freeze a board when it is first laid out, in the validator and in development, so a late mutation throws at the line that does it.
43. **Global verb names.** A film can replace an engine move for the whole bundle (item 10).
44. **Minor, performance.** `wordStateAt` walks every cue of the board for every word on every frame and rebuilds each cue's context (`wordState.ts:276-285`, `baseCtx` at `:234-238` sorts the affected list each call). It is fine today; a per-board index of cues by word would make it linear when boards get denser. Not a priority.

## Section 4. Proposed target architecture

The principle: keep the engine's data model (`Board`, `Block`, `RigKey`, verbs, validator, renderer) as the compile target and add the two layers it lacks, one above (a score that a storyboard maps to line for line) and one around (a host and command line so a film is data only). This is an extension of the one shared engine, not a new engine.

### Data flow

45. Song audio and Suno alignment go in; `kte songmap` writes the song map; a `score` (one card per lyric line) is compiled per format into `Board[]`; the validator checks the boards; the host renders them. A film folder holds only `song.json`, the song map, `look.ts`, `score/*.ts`, the audio, and renders. No Root, no compositions, no Remotion config, no `node_modules`, no copied tools.

### Song map ingestion (`tools/songmap/`, moved into the engine repo)

46. Keep the Python and librosa analysis, called by `kte songmap <film> [--alignment <path>]` (fixes 74.1). Changes:
    46.1. Split glued tokens on newlines and on closing brackets before building words, so "(One! Two! Three!)\n\nFour" is two lines and "ring!)" cannot swallow the next token's time (74.2, 74.5). Cap any word's end at the next word's start, and flag any word longer than 1.2 s in the report.
    46.2. Recompute each line's `start_s`, `end_s` and beat fields after all fixes and merges, in one final pass (74.3).
    46.3. Ad-libs: strip the parentheses from `text` in the data and set `adlib: true` on those words (74.6, and the case-freezing problem in item 33). The look decides how an ad-lib is set (smaller, second face); nothing draws brackets unless a card asks.
    46.4. Sections: when the lyric has no tags, infer them (repeated line groups are hooks; gaps over a threshold start a section) and mark them `inferred: true` for a one-glance check (74.4).
    46.5. Stable line keys: every line gets a key from its section and ordinal (`v1.3`, `hook2.1`), and identical hook lines share a `refrain` id. Scores address lines by key and words by text within the line, never by global index (item 29). A Radio Edit maps onto the same keys.
    46.6. An automatic ear-check list: words whose aligned start is far from any vocal onset, zero or very short words, overlong words. Twenty minutes of agent time per song today (brief 51.4) is mostly this.
    46.7. A schema version and a self-check (monotonic times, line id equals index) so the film never needs the guard that Ball's `song.ts` carries.

### Configuration: an engine handle instead of globals

47. Target shape:

```ts
export interface Look { palette: Palette; fonts: Record<FontKey, FontDef>; metrics: FontMetricsFile;
  voices: {lead: VoiceStyle; aside: VoiceStyle; adlib: VoiceStyle}; shadows: false | 'hero';
  guard?: RegExp[] }
export interface Stage { song: SongMap; format: FrameFormat; look: Look; rules: RuleProfile }
export const createStage = (s: Stage): Engine;          // no module state is set here
<TypeStage engine={engine} boards={boards} t={t} />      // and validateBoards(engine, boards)
```

48. A full rewrite that threads `engine` through every function touches every file. A staged route gets the benefit sooner: step one keeps the module bindings but puts them behind one `activate(engine)` call that `TypeStage` and the validator make at the top of each synchronous pass, and boards carry a reference to the engine they were compiled for, so two formats can live in one bundle and nothing depends on import order. Step two, later and optional, removes the bindings. The condition for step one is that film code stops reading engine values at import time, which the score format guarantees because cards are data.
49. Format-relative constants: express `SUNG_MIN_CAP`, margins, the framing box and every rule threshold as fractions of the safe box in `FrameFormat` (items 13, 14), and make the rig compose about the safe box centre by default.

### The score: a declarative per-line format the storyboard maps to directly

50. One card per lyric line. Rows are written as text, so the file reads like the storyboard and the compiler checks the text against the song map (a mismatch is a compile error naming the line, instead of a silently shifted index).

```ts
export interface Card {
  line: LineKey;                         // 'v2.1'
  rows: 'auto' | RowSpec[];              // ['NOW RAISE A GLASS TO', {text: 'ANT-MAN', size: 'hero'}, {text: 'mes amis,', voice: 'aside'}]
  place?: Placement;                     // default: composed in the safe box of the rest pose
  enter: 'auto' | Enter | Enter[];       // one for the card or one per row
  camera?: CameraCue;                    // how the rig arrives at this card's rest pose
  fate?: Fate;                           // what this card's type does when the next card lands
  accents?: Accent[];                    // moves on words already placed, addressed by word text
  vertical?: Partial<Card>;              // per-format override, only where needed
  raw?: (ctx: CompileCtx) => Partial<Board>;   // escape hatch for a set piece
}
export type Placement =
  | {zone: 'full' | 'top' | 'bottom' | 'left' | 'right'}                      // screen zone of the rest pose
  | {beside | under | above: Ref; match?: 'width' | 'cap'; gap?: number}      // relative to earlier text
  | {on: LetterRef}                                                            // anchor letter: {line: 'v2.1', word: 'ANT-MAN', letter: 'last', part: 'foot'}
  | {inside: LetterRef};                                                       // a counter
export type RowSpec = string | {text: string; size?: 'hero' | 'big' | 'body' | 'small'; voice?: 'lead' | 'aside' | 'adlib'; angle?: number};
export type Fate = 'stay' | 'margin' | Exit;                                   // margin: left behind small or sideways by the next camera move
```

51. What the compiler does so the author does not:
    51.1. **Layout.** Fits rows into the safe box of the card's rest pose for the target format (size words are fractions of safe width; `hero` fills 85 to 100 percent), then converts to world coordinates. This is `stations.ts` done once, in the engine, for any frame shape. A vertical compile re-breaks `rows: 'auto'` and re-fits given rows; `vertical:` overrides the rest.
    51.2. **Timing.** Every row or word lands on its own sung frame. A move's start is derived from its duration (itself derived from distance and the caps), so there are no literal frames in a score. Offsets are relative to words: `at: {word: 'FLY', frames: -2}`.
    51.3. **Camera.** `camera: roll(-90)`, `whip()`, `dive({into: letterRef})`, `step()`, `track()`, `hold()` compile to rig keys that end on the rest pose before the first sung word, with pre-set placement at the angle the camera will reach (Pulp Fiction item 26) handled by the compiler.
    51.4. **Fates and clearing.** `margin` leaves the old card in frame, sideways or cropped, after a roll; an `Exit` is scheduled so the old type is gone before the new type needs its space; the compiler reports a card that would land on live type.
    51.5. **Joins.** Sections are compiled as one film, so both sides of a join come from one `join` declaration. Hand-measured handoff pictures and per-module handoff ids go away for scored films (item 27).
    51.6. **`auto`.** `enter: 'auto'` and `rows: 'auto'` are filled by a rule-based director: pick from the effect catalogue weighted by the line's energy (the song map's 10 Hz energy), never the same family as the previous two cards, no move above a share cap, refrains escalate (swell, then a dive ladder). The purpose is that a new video compiles, validates and renders a watchable varied first cut before anyone writes a storyboard, and the storyboard becomes edits to it.
52. The output is ordinary `Board[]`, so hand-authored boards and scored cards can be mixed per section, old films keep working, and the validator needs no second code path.

### The effect library

53. One typed definition per effect, replacing the stringly registry:

```ts
export const rise = defineEffect({
  name: 'rise', kind: 'enter', family: 'mask', energy: 2,
  params: {dir: oneOf('up', 'down', 'left', 'right').default('up'), gap: px().default(6)},
  plan: (c) => ({frames: capFrames(...), endsAt: c.sungFrame}),      // timing derived, not authored
  modify: (c) => ({...}),                                             // as VerbDef.modify today
  ink: (c) => Pt[][],                                                 // everything it draws: letters, ghosts, marks
  reverse: 'sinkBehind',                                              // the exit that undoes it
});
```

    53.1. `kind` is `enter`, `exit`, `swap` (old and new linked), `accent` or `camera`. `family` (slide, mask, scale, turn, morph, type, stack, camera) is what the variety rule counts. `energy` lets the director and a rule match moves to the music.
    53.2. Params are declared, so an unknown or mistyped parameter is a compile error and a validator error (`unknown-param`), not a silent default (item 32). The union of effect names is generated from the definitions, so no casts.
    53.3. `defineEffect` throws on a duplicate name; `extendEffect(base, patch)` is the explicit form the engine's own `shove` and `hinge` need (item 10). Film-local effects must be named `film:<name>` and cannot shadow an engine name; the validator lists them as warnings so they get promoted or removed before a master is called done (item 36).
    53.4. `ink` is the single source for the validator's boxes, which closes the per-letter and ghost blind spot (item 12).
    53.5. **Composition.** Two generic wrappers remove most one-off verbs: `cascade(effect, {by: 'letter' | 'word' | 'row', stagger, order})` runs any entrance or exit per unit, and `reverse(effect)` gives any entrance as an exit. `Block.exit` then accepts any exit effect, not only `turn` (item 35).

### Camera

54. Keep the rig. Add:
    54.1. Helpers that exist only as habits today: `carriageReturn` (whip back and step down, Pulp Fiction item 18), `zoomLadder` for repeats (item 23), `beatPunch` (a short dive and return on a beat), `matchCut({from: letterRef, to: letterRef})` (a whip or dive that lands one letter exactly on the same letter of the next card).
    54.2. A perspective rig as an opt-in extension: two more pose fields (pitch and yaw) so the board itself can orbit and dolly during a transit, flat again at rest so every reading check still holds. The cheapest implementation to test is a CSS 3D transform on the outer stage element (Chrome applies perspective to an SVG element as a whole, only not to its children), with the validator projecting through the same homography as `turn3d.tsx` does for blocks. I have not tested this; sharpness of rasterised type at steep angles is the risk, so it needs a proof render and an angle limit (I would start at 35 degrees) before it is promised.

### Layout and legibility guarantees

55. Guarantees the compiler gives by construction, so they cannot fail review: every sung word inside the safe box at rest; cap height at or above the format's floor for its size class; no card landing on live type; type colour chosen against the ground it sits on.
56. Defaults that encode his standing preferences: `look.shadows: false` (remove the tier default at `TypeStage.tsx:66`; plates unshadowed), parentheses never drawn, the phonetic display guard generated from `song.json` `display` rather than retyped as regexes.

### Automatic checks

57. One rule profile for the type-only style, replacing the two opt-in families for scored films:
    57.1. `read-before-sung`: error when a word is readable (above the opacity and size thresholds, more than half in frame, unmasked by more than half) over 4 frames before its sung frame. The number is Fable's ruling, not his, so keep it a named constant.
    57.2. `entrance-variety`: error when two neighbouring cards use the same entrance family, or when any single move exceeds a share (I suggest 25 percent) of a section's entrances. Hooks are exempt when a refrain repeats on purpose.
    57.3. `rest-read`: the energy rule's "read at rest for 8 frames" (`transitRules.ts`), on for every board.
    57.4. `ink` checks from effect-declared ink, including letters and ghosts; `min-cap` for every displayed string including captions and ad-libs, as a fraction of the safe box; `contrast` between type and local ground.
    57.5. `unknown-param`, `film-effect` (warning), and `frozen-board` (item 42).
    57.6. Retire for this profile: `two-scales`, `level-run`, camera-angle `variety`, and the sheet camera rules, unless he asks for them. They remain available for the older films.

This does not replace watching motion, but it removes the blind build (item 26).
59. **Regression safety for the engine**: hash the node-rendered SVG of sampled frames of the example and each fixture, and fail `npm run check` when a hash changes without the snapshot being updated on purpose.

### Rendering

60. A host inside the engine repo (`host/index.ts`, `host/Root.tsx`) registers the compositions for whatever film `KTE_FILM` points at: one per section, the full film, and each format. `kte render <film> [--section v2] [--format short]` runs Remotion from the engine's own `node_modules`. This removes the relative alias, the worktree and symlink dance, the per-film lock file and the React pinning (item 30). Further:
    60.1. Sections render in parallel and are joined with the audio muxed once; a section's render is cached under a key of its compiled boards and the engine commit (stored in a sidecar, not in the file name), so a notes pass re-renders only what changed.
    60.2. Output names are generated, descriptive and never reused (`Ball Don't Lie - rough cut 14 - 21 November 2026.mp4`), which builds in his rule about never replacing a video he is watching.
    60.3. A master render refuses to run on a dirty engine tree and writes the engine commit into the film's pointer file itself.
    60.4. `kte where <film> 1:23` prints the card, row and effect on screen at that time, so a timestamped note maps to one place in the score.

### The new-video script

61. `kte new "<Title>" --audio <mp3> --alignment <json>` (the agent fetches the alignment with the Suno tool first, since a script cannot call it) should, in one run:
    61.1. Create `~/Experiments/<Title> Music Video/` with `audio/`, `analysis/`, `score/`, `renders/`, `reviews/`, git initialised, and the pointer file at the engine's real commit.
    61.2. Write `song.json` with detected candidates for him or the agent to confirm: hyphenated phonetic spellings, bracketed ad-libs, glued tokens.
    61.3. Run the song map, write its report and the ear-check list (item 46.6).
    61.4. Write `look.ts` from a default look (three faces, no shadows) and a score skeleton with one card per lyric line, `rows: 'auto'`, `enter: 'auto'`.
    61.5. Validate, and print the commands for a first rough cut in both 16:9 and 9:16. Both formats exist from the first minute; whether the vertical is rendered before the master is signed off stays his call.
    61.6. Fonts: measure one wide character set once (Latin with accents and punctuation) for the house faces, in node from the font files if that proves equal to the Chrome measurement, so a French lyric no longer forces a re-measure and a per-film copy.
    61.7. What stays hand-made: the look's colours, the storyboard edits to the score, and any `raw` set piece.

### Props: how to isolate them

62. Since the engine imports nothing from `props/`, isolation is a move, not a rewrite: `props/` becomes its own package beside the engine; the prop palette roles, `mono` and `figureLook` move out of `EngineConfig` into that package's config; `prop-clear`, `prop-size`, `keepClear` and the decor rules become a rule plug-in loaded only when a board has graphics; the object-drawing verbs (`explode` with `burst.tsx`, `bite`, `burn`, `smoke`, `count`, `brake`, `wipe`, `carry`) move to a graphic-effects module; panels split into grounds that type lives on (kept) and object panels (moved). The type-only profile then cannot reach a prop by accident.

## Section 6. Process changes that cut revision rounds

133. **The storyboard is the score.** Fable writes cards from the closed vocabulary instead of prose that builders translate into a thousand lines. It compiles and validates the moment it is written, so the storyboard gate reviews something that already moves, and the translation step, where much of the per-chunk gate looping lived, shrinks to set pieces.
134. **An effects reel he judges once.** Render every engine effect on sample text, in both formats, as one or two videos, and let him mark each move liked, disliked or conditional. He judges motion from video, not stills, and this spends that judgment once per effect instead of once per video. His marks become the director's weights. This is my suggestion, not something he asked for.
135. **Gate the film, not the chunk.** With a compiled score there are no chunk seams to reconcile, so gates can be: (a) song map and ear-check list; (b) auto rough cut compiles and validates; (c) storyboard edits, reviewed from the contact sheet and the sync table; (d) one full watch at speed with sound by a reviewer that can take video input, before he sees it; (e) him. Stage (d) exists because, on Paper Trail, nobody watched at speed with sound before he did.
136. **Builders get stills** from the node renderer (item 58) and are required to attach the contact sheet for their section to their report.
137. **Notes map to cards.** Each of his notes is logged against a card key with `kte where`, fixed in the score, and only that section re-renders, to a new file name.
138. **Engine hygiene as a rule of the workflow**: commit the working tree now, in reviewable pieces, and tag it; a video starts from a tag; engine changes during a video are committed with a fixture and a snapshot update before the next video starts; a master render refuses a dirty engine. Delete the stray worktree inside the repo only on his say.
139. **A promotion rule for effects**: a film may prototype a `film:` effect, and before its master is called done each one is either promoted into the engine with a reel entry or removed.
140. **Standing preferences live in the engine's defaults and one look file**, not in per-video briefs. When he states a standing rule, the change is made once in the default look or rule profile. Feedback he gives on one video stays that video's context unless he asks for a rule.
141. **Retire rules he never asked for from the default profile** (item 57.6) so that loops are spent only on things a viewer or he would notice. Keep every agent-made threshold labelled as ours.
142. **Fix the stale skill text** the brief lists (item 55 there) at the same time as the script lands, or the skill will keep sending builders to paths that do not exist.

## Section 7. What would make it a 9 or 10

143. A 9 is: `kte new` to a validated, varied, watchable rough cut in both formats with no hand-written code; a storyboard that is a score of about ten lines per lyric line; one typed effect library with exits, cascades, the film-stranded accents and the missing swaps in it; defaults that match his taste; the three checks his notes actually call for (in sync, readable, varied) as errors; stills for builders; a film that is data only and pinned to an engine tag.
144. A 10 adds: the perspective rig proven on a render; shared-letter match cuts and split and rejoin; a vertical that is a recompile plus a handful of overrides and passes the same variety bar as the master; section-cached rendering so a notes pass costs minutes; golden-frame protection for every finished film; and the effects reel marked up by him so the director's first cut is already to his taste.
145. Suggested order, with rough sizes that are my guesses and not measurements:
    145.1. Hygiene and defaults (small, about a day of agent work): commit and tag; shadows off; ad-libs stripped in data; duplicate registration made explicit; freeze boards; `read-before-sung` and `entrance-variety`; format-relative constants; safe-box rig centring.
    145.2. Host, `kte` commands and the new-video script, with the song map fixes (medium).
    145.3. Effect definitions with typed params, `ink`, `cascade`, `reverse`, and promotion of the film verbs (medium to large; the 20 existing moves mostly need wrapping, not rewriting).
    145.4. Score compiler and the auto director (the largest piece, and the one that pays most). Prove it by re-authoring one finished type-heavy section, for example Ball's hook, and comparing line counts and validator results against the hand-built boards before committing to it for a new video.
    145.5. Node stills and contact sheets, then golden frames (medium; one unverified dependency, item 58).
    145.6. Perspective rig and the remaining missing effects (medium; one unverified technique, item 54.2).
146. The main risk in this plan is the score compiler's automatic layout: fitting arbitrary lines into a rest pose without collisions is where a front end can produce dull or broken frames, which is why `raw` and per-card placement stay available and why 145.4 starts with a comparison on a known section. The second risk is scope: by his own earlier words, a rebuild that grows without a cost check is unwelcome, so each step above should land separately and leave the existing films validating unchanged.
