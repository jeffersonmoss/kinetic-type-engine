Written 21 November 2026 for a three-model council (Fable, Astra, Gemini Neon) that will audit the engine's architecture. These are organized notes compiled by a Claude (Opus 5.5) research agent from the code, the engine's KIT.md, the workflow skill, the video repos' review records and Jefferson's memory notes. ## Part A. What the engine is for

1. **The product.** Lyric music videos for Jefferson's own Suno songs: hype anthems (Ball Don't Lie, Luka's En Fuego, both basketball songs for his channel The Balls Report) and satire (Buy This IPO, Right Chip Wrong Huang, The DOGEfather, Sweet Distillation, Paper Trail). Each song becomes a 16:9 master at 1280 x 720, 30 fps, first; only after that, vertical 9:16 versions: a Short (about 1 to 3 minutes) and, for some songs, a full-length vertical (X gets full-length verticals; memory note `x-gets-full-length-vertical-videos.md`). They are published on his YouTube channels.
2. **The style.** Kinetic typography after "Pulp Fiction Typography" by Jarratt Moody: the lyric is set as blocks of type on one large world board; a camera rig turns, dives and tracks over it; each new line comes out of something already on the board. Study copy: `~/Experiments/Buy This IPO Music Video/reference/Pulp Fiction Typography - Jarratt Moody/` (the mp4 plus `frames/`, 462 frames at 8 fps). A frame-by-frame technique catalogue of that reference already exists in `~/Experiments/Buy This IPO Music Video/storyboard/Storyboard v3 - Pulp Fiction Motion.md`, Part 1.1 (items 8 to 36: anchor letter, seam slide, second voice typed small, nest in a counter, tip and spill, width docking, quarter roll that demotes the old block, upward tower, travelling read along a giant line, carriage return, words out of a wall, escalating zoom on repetition, slow roll onto a pre-set word, hairline that changes job, shelf and conveyor, build far away then roll in). Use it as the reference vocabulary.
3. **The speed goal, his words.** 21 November 2026: "Let's try to get this style of video down to a science so that we can crank them out more quickly with less revision rounds."

## Part B. Jefferson's goals for the engine (21 November 2026, verbatim)

4. "Let's try to get this style of video down to a science so that we can crank them out more quickly with less revision rounds."
5. "The props are the things that are always causing us to have to go back. And even then, they don't look great... there's a certain level of badness with those props that I can't really get around. So let's just try a video without using them at all. Not even a ball, just let's make it all kinetic typography."
6. "The movement of the text should tell the story, the scaling, the transformations, give it lots of energy, lots of variety, sliding in, turning in from the third dimension. Don't use drop shadows."
7. "Look to the pulp fiction typography, kinetic typography video as an example... look at how it connects letters and transforms them and introduces them and dismisses them."
8. "why are you having to set up a video workspace? When I thought we had built this engine so that we wouldn't have to set it up again each time, are we not fully reusing what we're building for each video?"
9. The audit goal: "set up the engine for maximum efficiency, quality and reusability", with "all the cool creative text transition effects built in".
10. **Scope.** Props are out of scope. He is switching to a no-props, all-typography style; props come back only after a new review. Part G lists which engine parts are prop-specific so the council can say whether to isolate them.

### Motion and variety (what he wants)

11. Buy This IPO cut, 16 November 2026: "All of the text is upright. There is none of the rotation and creative movement of the type from the pulp fiction example... the camera should be rotating, and words should be morphing into one another or expanding or revealing other words from behind them... This is all just right side up lettering that just appears on the left then appears on the right then appears on the left then appears on the right. There's no dynamic creativity to it."
12. Same night: "It's creative and playful the way it emerges always from the previous arrangement of text. The next line slides out or grows from it or replaces it or just interacts in creative ways that are analogues of physical playfulness."
13. Huang video, 16 November 2026: "Although there was more creativity in the animation, not much of it was in the behavior and animation of the typography itself."
14. Buy This IPO rough cut v3, 16 November 2026: "a lot of them are just very same with words just spelling out things over and over again in the same pattern... a lot of just words being spelled out letter by letter".
15. Sweet Distillation full-length vertical, 18 November 2026: "the text is very much almost just like a scrolling feed... It's just like words, just scrolling, like filling in from top to bottom... very same, same all the way through. Can you restore some of that energy?"
16. Same day, on 3D: "I'd like it to be able to handle words that like swing in from the third dimension... the face that was facing us becomes seen on edge and basically disappears, and the right edge pivots to become the new letter facing the camera... 17. Ball Don't Lie vertical, 21 November 2026 (memory `vertical-type-stays-lively.md`): "Make sure the type in the vertical version stays lively and doesn't get too static, all coming in the same way. Ours: no two neighbouring lines enter the same way (a quota Fable also set in the Sweet Distillation energy pass).
18. Graphics act out the lyric (IPO, 16 November 2026): "These lines should behave like what the lyrics are saying. Like, exploding doesn't mean stopping and turning into confetti, it means bursting upward."

### D2. Data flow, song to pixels

35. **Song map.** The skill's `SKILL/scripts/build_song_map.py <workspace>` reads `song.json` (song, audio path, `start_bpm`, `display` regex to on-screen spelling, `merge`, `fix` hand corrections) and `analysis/Suno lyric alignment.json` (from the Suno MCP `audio-lyrics-alignment`), runs librosa (beats with bar and beat-in-bar, onsets, 10 Hz energy) and writes `analysis/Song map.json` with words `{text, start_s, end_s, line}`, lines, sections, beats and energy. The video copies that file into its film project (`film/src/Song map.json`).
36. **configureEngine.** The video's `src/song.ts` maps the song map into `configureEngine(config)` (`ENGINE/engine/config.ts`): `words`, `lines`, `beats`, `energy`, `palette` roles (ground, shadow, paper, accent, accent2, highlight, light, plus prop roles body, detail, ground2), `fonts` (`{family, style, weight, upper, connectives}`), `metrics` (the measured metrics JSON), `wordRules` (connectives, emphasis, and `rule(i)`, a per-word house rule returning tier and WordOpts), `handoffs`, `guard` (banned spellings, enforced by `guardDisplay` throwing), `format` (`WIDE` or `SHORT_916`, `engine/format.ts`), `figureLook`. Every value is a module-level `let` binding (`FPS`, `WORDS`, `PALETTE`, `SPEED_CAP`, `BEAT_FRAMES` and so on), so one bundle holds one song and one frame size; the last call wins (KIT item 3).
37. **Boards.** Each chunk is a data-only TypeScript module exporting `START`, `END`, `BOARDS: Board[]` (and optionally `ELEMENTS`). It imports the song module first. A `Board` (`engine/types.ts`) is `{id, start, end, bg, exit, blocks, rig?, rigStart?, rules?, panels?, graphics?, paths?, ground?, carry?, handoff?, waive?, transitRules?, sheetRules?, carryOut?, smearType?, ...}`. A `Block` is a run of global word indices `[first, last]` with placement (`attach` code or `anchor`), sizing (justified rows by tier, fixed `cap`, or `stack`), and its required `emerge` (the move it comes out of something by), optional `verb` cues, `exit` (3D turn out), `turnSwap`, `style` per word, `showFrom`, `keepUntil`, `z`, `pin: 'screen'`, `on` (a panel). Excerpt from `ENGINE/example/boards.ts` (lines 45 to 55):

```ts
rules.push({id: 'kerb', job: 'seam', from: S1(300, 190), angle: 90, length: 880, t0: lineStart(1, 10), persistent: true});
add({words: range(1, 0, 2), attach: 'FREE', font: 'anton', cap: 60, width: 1200,
  anchor: {to: {rule: 'kerb', s: 0}, align: 'bl', angle: 90, offset: {x: 40, y: -12}},
  emerge: {move: 'seam', from: {rule: 'kerb', s: 0}, params: {side: 'R'}}});
add({words: range(1, 3, 3), attach: 'FREE', font: 'anton', cap: 300, width: 1200,
  at: {...S1(340, 230), angle: 90},
  emerge: {move: 'sprout', from: {word: w(1, 2), letter: 'last', part: 'foot'}}});
rig.push({t: lineStart(1, 12), kind: 'roll', by: -90, frames: 12, to: {frame: {point: C1}}, label: 'ROLL -90 (LIGHTS COME ON stays, sideways)'});
```

    37.1. Authoring is by hand: world coordinates, global word indices, song seconds, per-line rig keys. KIT 46.14 lists an optional "script lines with attach codes as a front end that produces blocks with anchors", recommended but not built. Per-chunk board files in the newest videos run 178 to 1,138 lines (Ball Don't Lie `film/src/boards/`, 8,142 lines in all).
38. **Layout** (`engine/layout.ts`). `layoutBoard(board)` lays out each block (tiers E0 to E3 with target sizes, row breaking, justification to width, connectives at half size), places blocks in world space by attachment code or anchor (`engine/letters.ts` resolves letter parts: flanks, foot, top, stemTop, tail, terminal, counter, centre), and is memoised in a `WeakMap<Board, BoardLayout>` (layout.ts line 387). Widths come from the metrics file, not the browser; words are drawn with SVG `textLength` and `lengthAdjust="spacingAndGlyphs"`, so layout is identical in node and Chrome (`engine/metrics.ts`).
39. **Camera** (`engine/rig.ts`, `engine/camera.ts`). The rig is the only camera, a pure function of (board, frame). Key kinds: roll, tilt, whip, step, dive, soar, track, crane, hold; targets frame a word, block, anchor or point by `cap`, `fill` or `cover`; `about` keeps a board point straight on screen; drift between keys (0.4 degrees and 1.5 percent zoom per second) unless `hold still`. Helpers in `engine/authoring.ts`: `fanTurn`, `quarter`, `runTrack`, `stackSteps`, `rigDiveInto`, `morphMarker`, `station`, `cameraFollow`, `tip`, `portal`. Chunk carries (`engine/carry.ts`: `slideUp`, `quarterCW`) fold a sheet move across a chunk boundary into the camera.
40. **Word life** (`engine/wordState.ts`). `wordStateAt(board, i, frame)` returns a `WordMod` (dx, dy, rot, sx, sy, opacity, per-letter mods, clip, colour, marks, ghosts, extend, turn). There is no default entrance: a block without `emerge` is hidden until its sung frame and the validator reports it. Every emergence and verb cue composes into the mod through `composeMod`. Then retirement (6 frames after the line ends, dims to 45 percent), cull, handoff clear, carry clear, board exit (`cut`, `fall`, `fade`, `leave`, `none`, `turn`), panel transforms and occlusion, ground-clear masks.
41. **Verbs** (`engine/verbRegistry.ts`). Everything that moves a word is a registered verb: `registerVerb(name, VerbDef)` with `modify(ctx)` returning a partial WordMod, plus optional hooks (`affects`, `replacesEnter`, `hiddenBefore`, `holds`, `binds`, `removesAt`, `displayFrom`, `lines`, `overlay`, `reserve`, `gradual`, `early`, `lead`). A block's `emerge` becomes a cue on the verb of that name (`emergeCue`, wordState.ts). The registry is one global `Map`; a later `registerVerb` with the same name replaces the earlier one for the whole bundle. Excerpt (`engine/verbRegistry.ts`):

```ts
export interface VerbDef {
  affects?: (cue: VerbCue, L: BoardLayout) => number[];
  replacesEnter?: boolean | ((cue: VerbCue, L: BoardLayout) => number[]);
  removesAt?: (c: VerbCtxBase & {i: number}) => number | null;
  modify?: (c: VerbWordCtx) => Partial<WordMod> | null;
  overlay?: (c: VerbCtxBase) => ReactNode;
  holds?: (cue: VerbCue, L: BoardLayout) => {word: number; until: number}[];
  lines?: (c: VerbCtxBase) => import('./lines').LineSeg[] | null;
  // ...
}
const REGISTRY = new Map<string, VerbDef>();
export const registerVerb = (name: string, def: VerbDef) => { REGISTRY.set(name, def); };
```

42. **Render** (`engine/TypeStage.tsx`, `engine/smear.tsx`, `engine/smearSampling.ts`, `engine/turn3d.tsx`). One SVG per frame: ground, then under the camera transform the under-graphics, rules, paths, blocks (per word `WordView`, whole word or per letter), verb overlays, over-graphics; then screen-pinned blocks, screen graphics, vignette. `SmearStage` adds motion blur on rig transits by averaging sub-frame samples, with words being read drawn once, sharp. 3D turns are drawn as projected strips of affine-mapped triangles because Chrome does not apply CSS perspective to SVG children (KIT 89.4).
43. **Validator** (`engine/validate.ts` 670 lines, `engine/validateRules.ts` 637, `engine/validateMotion.ts`, `engine/transitRules.ts`, `engine/motionCheck.ts`). `node tools/validate-boards.mjs <boards module> [EXPORT] [--step N] [--json] [--layout] [--at T]` bundles the boards module with esbuild into a temp folder and runs `validateBoards` over every frame of every word's reading interval, using the same `wordStateAt` as the renderer. About 66 rule ids, among them: `visible`, `margin`, `crop-half`, `sung-cap` (56 px cap), `stance`, `overlap`, `ink-overlap`, `transit-read`, `display-late`, `speed`, `min-life`, `emerge`, `emerge-anchor`, `arrival-pop`, `arrival-fade`, `anchor-hidden`, `empty-frame`, `two-scales`, `variety`, `level-run`, `load`, `decor-ink`, `line-life`, `line-linger`, `handoff`, `handoff-read`, `handoff-clear`, `carry`, `fast-move`, `still-camera`, `entrance-in-move`, `framing`, `energy-read`, `slide-early`, `morph-pin`, `ground-clear`, `picture`, `caption`, `guard`, `prop-clear`, `prop-size`. Rules are hard errors unless waived per board (`waive: [{rule, why, words?, block?}]`, KIT 83.3). Two rule families are opt-in per board because videos disagreed: `sheetRules` (the Huang and DOGE camera style) and `transitRules: 'energy'` (loosened checks while a word is in motion, added after the "scrolling feed" vertical; KIT 90).
44. **Fonts.** Videos load fonts in a browser-only `fonts.ts` (Google Fonts via `@remotion/google-fonts`). Metrics come from `tools/measure-fonts.mjs` (a page dumped in headless Chrome, outside any sandbox) and `tools/measure-outlines.mjs` (counters, ascender and descender ink from the TTFs). Only one font set has ever been measured in the engine; Ball Don't Lie re-measured it for French characters and keeps its own copy (`film/src/fonts/`).
45. **Vertical workflow.** `engine/format.ts` gives `WIDE` and `SHORT_916` (logical 720 x 1280, safe box x 48 to 612, y 102.4 to 1024); `engine/cut.ts` `sectionCut` maps song ranges to Short frames; `tools/splice-audio.mjs` and `tools/overlay-zones.mjs`; KIT "Vertical Shorts" and `ENGINE/VERTICAL SHORT WORKFLOW.md`. Nothing re-stages boards: every vertical is a new set of boards. Because config is global, a vertical needs its own Remotion entry point that imports the 16:9 song module first and then calls `configureEngine` again with the new format (and, for Ball Don't Lie, a different song map): `Ball Don't Lie Music Video/film/src/vertical-index.ts` and `film/src/vertical/song-radio.ts`, whose header lists five ordering rules builders must follow (for example "WIDTH and HEIGHT come from @engine (live: 720 and 1,280), never from ../song", and helpers that "hard-code the 640, 360 centre: do not use them").

### D3. Every text move and transition available today

Usage below is from a text search of each film's `src/` for the move or verb name (DOGEfather `film/src`, Sweet Distillation and Paper Trail `video/film/src`, Ball Don't Lie and En Fuego `film/src`); abbreviations DOGE, SD, PT, Ball, Fuego. A name found in source is a strong sign of use but not proof that it survived to the final cut. Buy This IPO and Right Chip Wrong Huang are not on this engine (they run on their own kits in `video/`), so they are not counted.

46. **Emergence moves** (block `emerge`, `engine/emergence/`; KIT 22):
    46.1. `seam`: rows slide out from behind a hairline, masked there; sung order or rigid; sides L, R, U, D. DOGE, SD, PT, Ball.
    46.2. `sprout`: scale from 0.08 about a named letter part, up to 6 percent overshoot. All five.
    46.3. `nest`: TYPE inside a counter, usually with a DIVE. Not found in the five.
    46.4. `door`: the old word swings open on an edge (or lifts as a lid) over the new block already underneath. Not found.
    46.5. `morph`: kept letters travel, others close and open; options `roll`, `arc`, `match: 'end'`, `pin`. All five.
    46.6. `shove` (push): new words push the old block, optional corner turn. DOGE, Fuego.
    46.7. `hinge` (swing or leaf, or on a panel). DOGE, PT.
    46.8. `fold`: old block folds shut on a rule, new opens from it. Not found.
    46.9. `stack`: words drop into rows justified to one measure; `stackSteps` for the camera. DOGE, Ball.
    46.10. `shelf` and `extrude`: words rise out of a rule (or a rule grown from a letter stroke). shelf DOGE, SD, Ball; extrude DOGE.
    46.11. `type`: letters fade in 2 frames apart. DOGE, SD, PT, Ball.
    46.12. `swell`: a repeat grows from the earlier word into place (also a cue). DOGE, SD.
    46.13. `tip`: rig tilts 15 degrees, retired words slide off. DOGE, SD.
    46.14. `portal`: counter fills with the next ground colour, rig dives through. SD.
    46.15. `drawer`: words slide out from behind an edge of a word or box. All five.
    46.16. `behind`: an opaque plate on the old words slides or hinges away, new words already behind. Not found as `behind(`.
    46.17. `slideFrom`: from a frame edge on a cruise-then-ease profile. SD, PT, Ball, Fuego.
    46.18. `rise`: from behind a line just under the word's own box (or a direction). All five, and the most used move by far (about 100 mentions in Ball, 97 in SD).
    46.19. `turn`: 3D turn in with perspective, about x or y, pivot edge or centre, per block, row, word or letter stagger; also `turnSwap` (revolving sign) and `exit: {style: 'turn'}`, board exit `turn`. SD, Ball (54 mentions), Fuego. Uncommitted.
    46.20. FAN placement (`attach: 'FAN'` with `fanTurn`). All five.

## Part G. What works well

76. **Validator in node with the renderer's own maths.** `wordStateAt` is shared by render and check, layout uses measured metrics so node and Chrome agree, and every board in every film validates to 0 errors before render. Builders without a browser can still check readability, margins, overlaps and speed frame by frame.
77. **Emergence as a required declaration.** Every block must say what it comes out of (`emerge` with an anchor that must resolve), which encodes his "emerges always from the previous arrangement" (item 12) as a hard rule rather than a hope.
78. **Verb registry.** New moves are added as one file without touching the core (most of phase 2 and the K additions went in this way), and emergences, exits and turns share one mechanism.
79. **Letter anatomy anchors.** Placing blocks on a named flank, foot, counter or stroke of an earlier letter gives the Pulp Fiction "anchor letter" and "words out of a wall" directly.
80. **The rig.** One camera per board as a pure function, with framing targets, `about` points, drift and named moves, carried across chunk seams by `carry`.
81. **3D turn.** Real projected perspective inside SVG, with the validator measuring the projected box (KIT 89).
82. **Opt-in additions.** Each video's new features were added as opt-in and checked against the example and fixtures line for line, so older films kept validating unchanged.
83. **Measured outcomes.** Ball Don't Lie built nine chunks to Fable sign-off and a full master in one working day; its rating loop reached 9.1 and 9.0 from two judges. Paper Trail reached a master in about 9 hours.

## Part I. Open questions for the council

89. **Architecture for reuse.** Is the present design (global configured singleton, a data-only boards module per chunk authored in world coordinates and global word indices, a global verb registry, opt-in rule families per board) the right base for an all-typography style that should be fast to author and need few revision rounds? In particular:
    89.1. Should configuration become an explicit context object (one per composition) instead of module-level bindings, so a 16:9 master and its vertical can share a bundle and nothing depends on import order?
    89.2. Should `layoutBoard` and `boardCues` memoisation stay keyed on object identity, given the stale-colour bug (item 69)?
    89.3. Should the verb registry refuse silent re-registration of an existing name (item 50.1)?
    89.4. Should the engine be pinned by commit (or a package), with every video addition committed before the next video starts (items 34, 73)?
90. **Efficiency.** Where is the per-video code going (8,142 lines of boards and 4,349 of drawings in Ball Don't Lie; 4,208 and 3,988 in En Fuego), and how much of the board code could a higher-level front end generate? For example a script or lyric-line format with per-line move choices and automatic placement, camera and timing (KIT 46.14), with hand-authored boards only for set pieces.
91. **Quality defaults.** Should the defaults encode his standing taste: no shadows by default (item 63), no parentheses by default (item 64), readable-before-sung as a validator error (item 66), an entrance-variety check (no two neighbouring lines entering the same way, and a cap on any one move's share; item 62), and a legibility floor for every displayed word including captions and echoes (item 65)?
92. **Is the library of text transitions complete?** Compare the moves in Part D3 with the Pulp Fiction catalogue (item 2) and the classic kinetic-type vocabulary. Candidates for the council to rule on, each with whether the engine has it today (our reading; please check):
    92.1. Letter morphs: yes (`morph`, with roll and pinned suffix).
    92.2. Words emerging from words: yes (`seam`, `sprout`, `drawer`, `shelf`, `extrude`, anchors on letter parts); `door`, `fold`, `nest`, `behind` exist but were not used in the five films.
    92.3. 3D flips and turn-ins: yes (`turn`, `turnSwap`, `turnBack`), uncommitted.
    92.4. Camera orbit and dolly: roll, tilt, dive, soar, track, crane exist; a true 3D orbit or dolly (the board itself seen in perspective) does not.
    92.5. Scale punches: `grow`, `swell`, `stretch`, `repeat`; no beat-synced punch or bounce verb in the engine (Ball and En Fuego wrote `pulse`, `dropBounce`, `bump` locally).
    92.6. Slides: `slideFrom`, `rise`, `slideAlong`; directional `glide` and `arcIn` exist only in the films.
    92.7. Cascades: per-letter stagger exists in `turn`, `vanish`, `type`, `seam` order; no general per-letter cascade entrance (drop, rotate or scale each letter in turn).
    92.8. Type as shapes: letter art (`replace`, `hole`, `ring`, functions), picture words; no type set on a path or curve, no words forming a shape.
    92.9. Match cuts on shared letters: partly (`morph` keeps shared letters; `relight` reuses a word); no cut or camera move keyed to a shared letter across boards.
    92.10. Word swaps: `morph`, `turnSwap`, `door`; no slot-machine or split-flap swap except a staggered turn on plates.
    92.11. Stacking and unstacking: `stack`, `shoveUp`, `tip`; no reverse (unstack) move.
    92.12. Typewriter: `type`.
    92.13. Split and rejoin: none (a word splitting at a letter, halves sliding apart and closing).
    92.14. Masks and reveals: seam, drawer, shelf, behind, portal, clearing disc; no wipe-reveal across a word by an arbitrary edge or a letterform used as a mask for the next ground.
    92.15. Dismissals: vanish, closeAway, tumble, sinkBehind, slideAlong, turn out, board exits; Pulp Fiction's habit of leaving old text as a sideways margin note is covered by retirement and rolls.
