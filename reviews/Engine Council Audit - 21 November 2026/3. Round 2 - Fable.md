Written 21 November 2026 after reading Astra's and Neon's round 1 audits in full and re-reading my own. Limits, first:

1. I still have rendered and watched nothing. Everything here is from code, validator runs and the written record.
2. New checks made this round, all in the working tree: the `stack` direction bug Astra reported (confirmed, see item 19); `slideFrom`'s dummy anchor that Neon reported (confirmed at `engine/authoring.ts:93-94`); the share of bare-point emergence anchors in Ball's board sources (133 point, 35 word, 18 rule, 13 block); the `glide` usage my round 1 count missed (131 mentions in Ball's boards); the morph pin's definition (`engine/morphPin.ts:1-9`); the silent pass for unknown handoff ids (`engine/validate.ts:584`).

## Section 1. Agreements (each as one decision)

5. **Keep the core.** Retain `wordStateAt` as the one state function for render and check, measured-metrics layout, letter-anatomy anchors, the planar rig, projected 3D turns and the existing `Board` API. No new engine.
6. **Build a declarative per-line authoring layer that compiles to boards**, with hand-authored boards as an escape hatch for set pieces. All three named this the largest gap.
7. **Compile one film timeline.** Chunk previews, seam previews, the validator and the final render all read ranges of the same compiled timeline, so `Full.tsx` board copies and hand-measured handoff pictures end.
8. **Replace the module-level configuration with an engine instance** so the master and its verticals share one bundle with no import-order rules.
9. **Stop keying correctness on object identity.** Compiled boards are immutable; a late mutation is an error, not a silently stale layout.
10. **Make the effect registry typed and closed to silent override.** Duplicate names are rejected; an intentional extension (the engine's own `shove` and `hinge`) uses an explicit base.
11. **Give the validator the same ink the viewer sees**: per-letter motion, ghost copies and masks, plus contrast against the actual ground.
12. **Add a general first-readable-frame check against the vocal onset** for every effect, with the threshold a named constant that is not presented as his number.
13. **Report entrance families per section.** (Whether repetition is an error or a report is still open, item 27.)
14. **Defaults: no drop shadows anywhere (words and plates), no Suno parentheses on screen**, with ad-lib membership kept as data.
15. **Fix song import**: explicit input paths, split glued tokens before attaching punctuation, apply fixes and merges before deriving line bounds and beats, flag estimated timings and unlabelled sections, emit stable ids, produce a short ear-check list.
16. **One new-video command** that produces a data-only film (manifest, song map, seeded storyboard, look) with no copied config, tools, metrics or `node_modules`, pinned to an engine release.
17. **Capture the working tree as a release now**, and make an unpinned or dirty engine unable to produce a master.
18. **Isolate props in an optional package**, promote the proven film-local moves, add a general stagger, split and rejoin, and a shared-letter match cut; a perspective orbit and dolly come later and stay optional. Review happens on video at speed with sound, to new file names, and both formats compile from shared intent with explicit per-shot overrides.

## Section 2. Where I change my mind

19. **Emergence is not the checked invariant I said it was (my round 1 item 20).** Astra and Neon are right. `emerge` is optional in the type, a bare point is a valid origin, and `slideFrom` supplies `{point: {x: 0, y: 0}}` (`engine/authoring.ts:94`, whose comment calls it the frame centre). In Ball's board sources 133 of 199 anchors are bare points; Astra counts 415 of 513 instantiated blocks. The validator proves that an anchor resolves, not that the new line comes out of type on the board. The Pulp Fiction relationship is therefore mostly held by coincidence of coordinates, and it will not survive a reflow. This raises the value of the score layer, where the source is a named glyph.
20. **My entrance count was incomplete (my round 1 items 15 and 34).** I counted literal `move:` declarations and missed helper-built entrances, notably `glide` (131 mentions in Ball's boards). Astra's instantiated count for the master is 223 `turn`, 120 `rise`, 111 `glide` out of 513. My conclusion that a few moves carry nearly everything stands (three moves, 88 percent), but "`rise` is 47 percent of Ball" is wrong as a description of what is on screen, and "the rules push builders to `rise`" is no longer the right diagnosis for Ball. The diagnosis is concentration in whatever is safest, which is now `turn`.
21. **The unit of authoring should be the transition between two lines, not the line (Astra).** My card had separate `enter` and `fate` fields, which leaves the author coordinating the old line's lifetime with the new line's arrival, the same four-edit problem as today in fewer lines. Astra's contract is better: the compiler resolves source and destination together, keeps the source alive until transfer, schedules backwards from the sung frame and reserves the destination's footprint. I adopt it (Section 6).
22. **Hooks are motifs.** Astra's "a hook is a reusable motif with occurrence-level variations" is stronger than my `refrain` id. Ball's hook builder multiplying one declaration into many blocks shows the need already exists.
23. **The audio edit and the aspect ratio are independent inputs (Astra).** I treated verticals as a format question only. The Radio Edit shows otherwise, and `engine/cut.ts` (integer frame ranges) and `tools/splice-audio.mjs` (one crossfade with its own timing) do not share an edit list. One edit list should drive both.
24. **Untyped params are already causing a real bug, not only a risk.** Astra's `stack` finding is confirmed: `emergeCue` sets `params.from` to the anchor object (`engine/wordState.ts:56`), `stack` reads `params.from` as a direction (`engine/emergence/stack.ts:13-17`), no branch matches an object, and the motion falls through to the rightward case (`:66`). A stack can never drop from above through `emerge`. This moves typed effect definitions up my list.
25. **Authoring helpers read configuration when called (Neon).** `stackSteps`, `runTrack`, `portal` and `cameraFollow` read `WORDS`, `FPS`, `WIDTH` and `PALETTE` at call time. My staged "activate the engine at the top of each pass" route is therefore only safe if boards are built inside a compile call with the engine active, never at module load. I keep the staged route with that condition written in.
26. **What a 10 means.** My round 1 list was features. Astra's is outcomes on two contrasting finished songs (share of lines without custom code, zero film-only seam patches, owner correction rounds per master, time per finished minute). That is the right test and I adopt it, with the numbers labelled as proposed targets.

Smaller adoptions: Neon's renderer faults (overlays and `over` graphics drawn only in the blur pass; whole-word to per-letter switch mid-hold; one 6 px shadow offset for every size) go on the fix list; Neon's `missing-glyph` check at setup; Astra's point that the morph pin is relative to the board through the camera, so a screen pin must be a separate declared mode; unknown handoff ids should be an error.

## Section 3. Disagreements that remain

27. **Entrance variety: blocking error or report?**
    27.1. My position in round 1, and Neon's: error on same family twice running and on a 25 percent share.
    27.2. Strongest other side (Astra): a repeating hook can be exactly right, none of these thresholds is his, and a rule that blocks on taste recreates the gating problem that produced the scrolling-feed vertical, only pointed at a different move.
    27.3. Evidence: Fable's own record says its gating caused that vertical (brief 62). Against pure reporting: Ball's master carries 271 warnings (Astra's run), so a warning is invisible in practice.
    27.4. Resolution: a budget, not a ban. The compiler reports families per section; a neighbouring repeat or a share over the budget is an error unless the card or motif says `repeat: 'intended'`, which costs one word and is listed in the review summary. The same budget is a hard constraint on any automatic pick. He never sees a number; he sees a reel if he wants one.
28. **How much to rebuild at once.**
    28.1. Neon: seven workspace packages, context threaded through every function, `WordMod` replaced by a new per-word evaluation with a mandatory hull, and emerge, verb, exit and board exit unified under one plugin contract.
    28.2. Strongest case for it: half measures leave two systems, the `tn.stand` stand-in and the split between emergence and verbs are real sources of error, and a clean contract is what makes the validator trustworthy.
    28.3. My position, with Astra: keep `Board` and `WordMod` as the compile target, add `ink` to effects, unify at the authoring level first. Evidence: every one of the 43 engine files reads the live bindings; five films validate on the current API; his standing instruction is to reuse and improve, and to be told before feedback grows into a rebuild.
    28.4. Resolution: Astra's migration order. Release, then scaffold and compile one verse, hook and seam into existing boards, then move configuration into instances with fixture parity, then widen the ink evaluator where effects need it. One repo with enforced import boundaries (core, effects, score, validate, props) gives Neon's separation without a packaging project.
29. **Speed cap on type.**
    29.1. Neon: no speed cap on intentional kinetic transits.
    29.2. Strongest case: the cap stretched moves until words landed early or everything moved at one tempo, and the energy rules already exempt type.
    29.3. My position: the cap's number is ours, but its source is his sentence about moving things "only fast enough that the eye is still able to follow" (brief 19). Keep the existing bounded exemption (12 frames of free transit, then 8 frames read at rest, `engine/transitRules.ts`) as the default for all type, and keep the cap on anything that is being read while it moves.
    29.4. Resolution: adopt the energy model as the single profile; do not remove the cap outright.
30. **Automatic first cut.**
    30.1. My round 1 proposal: `enter: 'auto'` filled by a rule-based director so a new song renders at once.
    30.2. Strongest other side (Astra): the hand-made work is the music decision, and generic automatic motion is exactly what he has rejected three times; a first cut made of defaults will anchor the storyboard.
    30.3. Resolution: the scaffold seeds rows and timing only. Unfilled transitions compile in a draft mode that is watermarked and usable for checking sync and layout; a master render refuses while any transition is unfilled. The effects reel I proposed in round 1 stays as a suggestion for calibrating taste once, not as a requirement.
31. **Automatic vertical restaging.**
    31.1. Neon: a solver that re-wraps to two words a row, redirects wide runs into diagonals and spines, and needs overrides on 10 to 20 percent of lines.
    31.2. Strongest case: without automation the vertical is a rewrite, and rewrites are where energy was lost.
    31.3. My position, with Astra: try the declared connection, row breaks and camera against the real safe box, try a small set of alternatives, and otherwise fail with a named conflict for the author. No fixed words-per-row rule; fit decides. The override share is unknown until measured on one section.
    31.4. Resolution: build the fail-loud version, measure the override share on a hook and a verse, then decide how much solver is worth building.
32. **Fixed numeric thresholds as errors (4 frames early, 56 px for all text, contrast 4.5).** Neon states them as errors. I agree with the checks and not with presenting the numbers as settled: 4 frames is a Fable ruling, the backlog says 3, and 4.5 is the web standard for body text where large display type is usually held to 3. Resolution: named constants in one profile file, each labelled with its source; contrast errors below 3 and warns below 4.5.
33. **Renderer fallback to WebGL or canvas for heavy turns (Neon).** I would not add a second renderer. The single SVG is what keeps masks, draw order and the validator in agreement. If staggered turns are slow, cut strip counts.

## Section 6. Recommended architecture in one page

55. **Modules, in one repo with enforced import boundaries.** `song` (import, ids, ear-check list). `score` (storyboard types and the compiler). `effects` (typed catalogue). `core` (today's layout, rig, word state, stage, smear, turn). `validate` (profile, ink, sync table). `host` (the Remotion entry and compositions). `cli` (`kte`). `props` (optional, never imported by the others).
56. **Data flow.** Audio, alignment and `song.json` become a song map with stable ids. The storyboard plus an edit list plus a format compile to an immutable film timeline of boards. The validator and the host both read ranges of that timeline.
57. **Per-line authoring format.** One entry per lyric line, written as a transition from something already on the board:

```ts
line('v2.3', {
  rows: ['THE MAN', {text: 'CAN FLY', size: 'hero'}],     // text checked against the song map
  from: glyph('v2.2', 'AMIS', {letter: 'last', part: 'flankR'}),   // the source landmark
  enter: seam({side: 'R'}),                               // typed; lands on the sung frame by default
  old: 'margin',                                          // or an exit, or 'stay'; the compiler owns the source's lifetime
  camera: fan(-12),                                       // intent; keys are derived
  accents: [punch('FLY')],
  portrait: {rows: ['THE MAN', 'CAN', {text: 'FLY', size: 'hero'}]},
})
motif('hook', {...}, {occurrences: {2: {enter: turnIn({axis: 'x'})}, 4: {camera: zoomLadder()}}})
raw('v4.7', (ctx) => boards)                              // a hand-built set piece
```

    The compiler resolves source and destination together, fits rows to the format's safe box at the rest pose, schedules back from the vocal onset, derives camera keys, plans each join once, and reports a named conflict when a line cannot fit. No world coordinates, global indices or literal frames appear in a storyboard.
58. **New-video command.** `kte new "<Title>" --audio <mp3> --alignment <json>` creates the film folder under `~/Experiments`, copies the audio once, writes `song.json` with detected spellings and ad-libs to confirm, builds the song map and ear-check list, checks every lyric character against the font metrics, writes the default look and a storyboard seeded with rows and timing for every line, registers wide, Short and full-vertical profiles, and records the engine tag. Reruns never overwrite authored lines. Draft renders work at once; a master render needs every transition filled and a clean, tagged engine.
59. **Defaults.** No drop shadows on words or plates. Parentheses never drawn; ad-libs carried as a flag and set in the look's ad-lib voice. Display guard generated from `song.json`. Camera composed about the safe box. One rule profile for type-only films.
60. **Checks.** Errors: lyric coverage, broken source links, banned spellings, missing glyphs, unknown parameters, read before sung beyond the profile's lead, not readable at the sung frame, below the size floor for any displayed text, contrast below the floor, outside the safe box during its read, ink collisions from effect-declared ink, seam jumps in position, angle, scale or ground, variety over budget without a stated intent. Reports: the sync table, entrance families per section, film-local effects still in use. Every threshold is a named constant with its source.
61. **Versioning.** The engine is tagged; a film records the tag and renders only from it; golden frames guard the example, fixtures and each finished film; a new move made during a video is promoted with a fixture or removed before that video's master; outputs get generated, never reused, file names.

## Section 7. Score if this were built well

62. **8.5 out of 10.** With Section 6 built and proven, setup is one command, most of a film is a storyboard a reviewer can read, the notes he repeats (shadows, parentheses, sync, sameness, small type) are prevented or caught before he watches, and a vertical is a recompile with overrides.
63. What would still keep it below 10:
    63.1. It is unproven until two contrasting songs have gone through it with measured results. Astra's proposed targets are the right test: most lines without custom code, no film-only seam patches, two or fewer owner correction rounds per master.
    63.2. Automatic layout of arbitrary lines from a named glyph is the hard part, and it can produce dull or cramped frames. How often an author must override, in wide and especially in portrait, is not known.
    63.3. Taste is not checkable. A film can pass every rule and still need a better transition; the variety budget measures families, not whether the motion acts out the lyric.
    63.4. The builders and I still cannot watch motion with sound inside a sandbox, so the gap between what passes and what a viewer sees narrows but does not close. A reviewer that takes video, and he himself, remain the real gate.
    63.5. Alignment quality from Suno sets a ceiling on sync that no engine check removes; the ear check reduces it.
    63.6. The perspective camera, outline morphs and type on a path are unproven techniques in this renderer.
