# Engine council, round 2: Neon consensus

## Part 1. Agreements

All three auditors (Fable 5/10, Astra 6/10, Neon 5.5/10) converge on 15 concrete decisions.

1. **Keep the deterministic math core.** Retain `wordStateAt`, pre-measured font metrics with SVG `textLength`, letter-anatomy anchors, the pure-function camera rig, the Node validator, and the single-SVG stage with projected 3D turns. Extend the existing engine rather than rewriting from scratch.
2. **Commit, clean, and tag the engine immediately.** Commit the 31 modified files and ~47 untracked files currently stranded in the working tree (`turn`, `turn3d`, `transitRules`, `spread`, `wah`, `groundClear`, `morphPin`), resolve the stray detached worktree (`Kinetic Type Engine HEAD check/`), tag a real release, and forbid master renders against a dirty engine tree.
3. **Add a declarative per-line authoring layer that compiles to `Board[]`.** Move everyday authoring above raw world coordinates, global word indices, and literal frame numbers, while keeping a `raw` board escape hatch for bespoke set-pieces and backward compatibility with existing films.
4. **Address lyrics by stable section, line, and word-occurrence IDs.** Replace global numeric word indices in authored choreography so alignment fixes, merges, Short cuts, and Radio Edits never shift downstream references.
5. **Eliminate import-order-dependent global configuration.** Stop relying on module-level `configureEngine` singletons so 16:9 master, 9:16 full vertical, and 9:16 Short can live in one bundle without duplicate entry points or fragile import checklists.
6. **Eliminate silent stale-cache mutations on `Board` objects.** Replace mutable-object `WeakMap` caching (12 instances across `layout.ts`, `rig.ts`, and `wordState.ts`) with immutable compiled boards or frozen board objects so late edits can never render stale colors (the Midnight-on-Midnight bug).
7. **Isolate props into an optional separate package.** Move `props/`, prop palette roles (`body`, `detail`, `ground2`, `figureLook`, `mono`), object-drawing verbs (`explode`/`burst.tsx`, `bite`, `burn`, `smoke`, `count`, `brake`, `wipe`, `carry`), object panels, and prop validator rules out of the pure-typography core.
8. **Encode Jefferson's standing taste in engine defaults.** Turn drop shadows OFF by default on both words (`TypeStage.tsx:66`) and all plate branches (`TypeStage.tsx:113, 141, 165`), and strip Suno ad-lib parentheses in song-map data while preserving `adlib: true` metadata for intentional styling.
9. **Fix all song-map ingestion bugs in one pipeline.** Accept explicit audio and alignment paths, tokenize internal newlines and closing brackets before word assembly, clamp glued-punctuation duration outliers (such as `ring!)` stretching 12.5 seconds), apply fixes and merges before computing line bounds and beats, infer missing sections, and generate an automatic ear-check shortlist.
10. **Make layout, framing, and legibility checks format-aware against the actual asymmetric `safeBox`.** Replace fixed logical pixels (`SUNG_MIN_CAP`, `GIANT_SPAN`, `DEFAULT_W`) and symmetric framing boxes (`BOX_W`, `BOX_H` in `config.ts:133-134`, `rig.ts:84`, `chain.ts:64`) with `safeBox`-relative math so 9:16 restaging and framing work without waivers or 77 px centering offsets.
11. **Unify validation and close its visual blind spots.** Retire the conflicting `sheetRules` vs `transitRules: 'energy'` toggle split for new pure-type videos; add vocal-sync checks (earliest readable frame vs sung onset), actual backdrop contrast checks, and effect-reported ink geometry covering per-letter motion, ghosts, complex masks, and active 3D turns.
12. **Harden and promote the effect library.** Replace untyped `Record<string, unknown>` verb params and stringly casts with typed effect definitions, replace silent duplicate registration with explicit extension, and promote proven film-local moves (`glide`, `arcIn`, `dropBounce`, `tiltTo`, `layDown`, `pulse`/`bump`, `jolt`, `squash`, `ride`, `snap`) into the shared engine.
13. **Promote exits, cascades, and line-to-line recipes to first-class primitives.** Open `Block.exit` beyond `turn`, build generic `cascade` (per-letter/word/row stagger) and `reverse` wrappers, and ship one-call recipes for Pulp Fiction transitions (shared-letter match cuts, split-and-rejoin, margin-spine demotion).
14. **Compile the full film as one timeline with unified seams.** Remove per-chunk hand-measured pixel handoffs and manual `Full.tsx` seam patches by generating both sides of every boundary from one compiled timeline and one sample-accurate audio/picture edit list.
15. **Ship a one-command new-video setup and non-destructive rendering workflow.** Replace copied `node_modules`, relative `../../` aliases, `register-chunk.py`, and copied wrappers with a single CLI (`kte new`, `kte check`, `kte render`), auto-incrementing output filenames (never overwriting an open QuickTime file), master-first sign-off, and mandatory playback-with-sound review before Jefferson watches.

## Part 2. Where I change my mind

Reading Fable's and Astra's full-codeback audits (especially on files truncated from my Round 1 input) changed my mind on eight specific points.

16. **Drop the 7-package `@kte/*` monorepo split in favor of one repo with an engine-hosted runner.** In Round 1, I proposed splitting the engine into seven `@kte/*` npm packages (`@kte/core`, `@kte/transitions`, `@kte/choreo`, etc.) and threading `EngineContext` through every file up front. Fable's single-repo host design (`host/Root.tsx` driven by `KTE_FILM`, item 60) and Astra's incremental migration order fit Jefferson's warning against expensive rebuilds (Brief 30) far better. Clean internal directories inside one tagged repo give zero per-video boilerplate immediately with a fraction of the packaging friction.
17. **Author transitions as linked pairs of lines, not isolated line cards with a `relation` tag.** Astra's measurement of Ball Don't Lie's instantiated blocks was decisive: 415 of 513 blocks name an arbitrary `point`, while only 50 name a word, 31 a rule, and 17 a block (Astra Section 2). My Round 1 `ChoreoLine` still treated `enter`, `exit`, and `relation` as separate fields, which leaves the builder coordinating source lifetime, reveal geometry, and camera landing manually. Adopting Astra's explicit `source` / `leave` landmark pairing plus typed recipes that own both outgoing and incoming lifetimes solves the real cause of coordinate sprawl.
18. **Separate `source` anchor from `direction` in effect schemas.** Astra reproduced a concrete bug I had no visibility into (`engine/wordState.ts:56` and `engine/emergence/stack.ts:13, 61`): `emergeCue` overwrites `from: "above"` with the anchor object, silently making `stack` travel right instead of down. My Round 1 `TransitionPlugin` did not explicitly separate `source`, `direction`, `pivot`, and `timing`; it must.
19. **Use explicit `extendEffect(base, patch)` instead of a blanket ban on duplicate registration.** In Round 1, I argued `registerVerb` should simply refuse re-registration. Both Fable and Astra verified that built-in `shove` (`engine/verbs.tsx:566` + `engine/emergence/shove.ts:75`) and `hinge` (`engine/verbs.tsx:590` + `engine/emergence/hinge.ts:33`) intentionally wrap earlier registrations. Throwing on silent overwrite while adding `extendEffect` preserves engine semantics cleanly.
20. **Stop treating entrance-variety quotas as hard build-blocking errors.** In Round 1, I made consecutive same-family entrances and >25% move share hard errors in `KINETIC_V2`. Astra's distinction between technical blockers and taste diagnostics (Section 6.4) and Fable's hook exemption persuaded me: deliberate repetition on a chorus or motif can be right, and over-strict validator rules are what caused `two-scales` to be waived 10 times across Ball and En Fuego (Fable 28). Variety should be a prominent dashboard + warning (and soft guard on verses), not a hard block.
21. **Express legibility floors relative to `safeBox`, not fixed 56 px.** In Round 1, I carried over `minCapPx: 56` as a fixed default. Fable (`validate.ts:67`, `validateRules.ts:52-53`, `rig.ts:84`) and Astra (`chain.ts:64`, `format.ts:7`) proved that fixed pixel thresholds break `SHORT_916` (where safe width is 564 px and the safe box is asymmetric). All sizing, framing, and margin constants must derive from `format.safeBox`.
22. **Add Node SVG regression hashes and a one-time Jefferson Effects Reel.** In Round 1, I focused only on Chrome `--draft` renders, overlooking that sandboxed builders cannot launch Chrome (Brief 71). Fable verified that `engine/` only imports Remotion in `engine/time.ts:2` (item 23), making Node `react-dom/server` SVG hashing cheap and immediate for regression tests. Fable's Effects Reel (item 134) is also a smarter way to capture Jefferson's motion taste once per effect rather than discovering dislikes mid-video.
23. **Support both `board` and `screen` pin modes in `morph`, and unify picture/audio edit lists.** Astra caught two real engine gaps I missed: `engine/morphPin.ts:3` measures drift in board space rather than screen space, and `engine/cut.ts` vs `tools/splice-audio.mjs` use incompatible edit/crossfade conventions. Both belong in the core architecture.

## Part 3. Disagreements that remain

24. **Disagreement 1: Rule-based `enter: 'auto'` director (Fable) vs human/agent-authored line-pair direction (Astra).**
    24.1. *Strongest case for Fable's position:* `enter: 'auto'` weighted by 10 Hz song energy and non-repeating families lets `kte new` compile and render a watchable, varied rough cut in minutes (Fable 51.6, 61.4), turning storyboarding into faster editing rather than blank-page writing.
    24.2. *Strongest case for Astra's position:* Pulp Fiction typography works because motion acts out the lyric's meaning and physical word anatomy (Brief 12, 18; Astra 6.2). Energy-weighted random variety produces generic motion soup that looks active without serving the song, and anchors reviewers to mediocre default choices.
    24.3. *My position and deciding evidence:* Fable himself flags automatic layout collisions as the main risk of dull or broken frames (Fable 146), and Jefferson's feedback repeatedly rejects generic templates (Brief 11, 14, 15, 18). At the same time, automated safe-box row wrapping and vocal timing (`rows: 'auto'`) have zero artistic downside and eliminate the worst tedium.
    24.4. *Resolution:* Separate layout/timing automation from creative choreography. Make `rows: 'auto'` and vocal-synced timing the default, and keep `enter: 'auto'` strictly an opt-in `--auto-draft` preview mode that stamps generated moves as `auto('turn')` in the score file so the storyboard pass explicitly replaces or approves every hero line before production.

25. **Disagreement 2: Staged `activate(engine)` + `WeakMap` freeze (Fable) vs pure `EngineContext` + compiled timeline from day one (Astra, Neon).**
    25.1. *Strongest case for Fable's position:* Threading `engine` through all 43+ files under `engine/` is a wide refactor (Fable 48). Calling `activate(engine)` before each synchronous render/validate pass and freezing boards at layout time fixes multi-format bundles and stale-cache bugs in roughly one day without risking a rebuild.
    25.2. *Strongest case for Astra's and my position:* `engine/authoring.ts` reads globals at module load time (`stackSteps:67-70`, `runTrack:122-127`, `portal:54-63`, `cameraFollow:147-158` closure cache), and film code like `hook.ts` captures `BEATS` at import (Fable 40). Module-level globals also break if React 19 concurrent rendering or parallel multi-format compilation runs in one Node process.
    25.3. *My position and deciding evidence:* Both concerns are real. The code in `engine/authoring.ts` proves `activate(engine)` alone will not protect helpers that capture bindings at call time, yet refactoring all legacy boards on day one delays real video payoff.
    25.4. *Resolution:* Make the new compiler (`compileScore(ctx, score)`) 100% pure and immutable from day one, emitting frozen `CompiledBoard[]` with pre-computed layouts and indexed cues (immediately bypassing the 12 `WeakMap`s and `authoring.ts` load-time reads for all new videos). Use a temporary synchronous `withEngine(ctx, fn)` bridge strictly at the legacy `TypeStage` / `validateBoards` boundary, then remove module-level `let` bindings in Phase 2 once the first new video ships.

26. **Disagreement 3: Sandboxed Node raster stills (`resvg`, Fable) vs Chrome production-browser motion previews (Astra, Neon).**
    26.1. *Strongest case for Fable's position:* Sandboxed builders cannot launch Chrome (Brief 71). Rendering SVG in Node and rasterizing with `resvg` gives builders instant contact sheets (`kte sheet`, Fable 58) so they stop building blind.
    26.2. *Strongest case for Astra's and my position:* Jefferson explicitly refuses to judge motion from stills (Brief 27). Furthermore, `engine/TypeStage.tsx` relies on `textLength` with `lengthAdjust="spacingAndGlyphs"` (lines 75, 98), which `resvg` does not reliably honor (Fable acknowledges this as unverified, item 58), risking false confidence from stills that disagree with Chrome.
    26.3. *My position and deciding evidence:* Node SVG serialization (`react-dom/server`) works out of the box because `engine/time.ts:2` is the only Remotion dependency, so structural SVG snapshots are guaranteed accurate, while rasterizing `textLength` in `resvg` is unverified.
    26.4. *Resolution:* Split the two jobs. Ship zero-dependency Node SVG serialization immediately for golden-SVG regression hashes (and optional contact-sheet stills using glyph outlines from `measure-outlines.mjs` only after a 1-hour parity test against Chrome). Keep fast 480p Chrome `--draft` renders with audio muxed as the mandatory motion gate for the main agent and three-judge review panel.

27. **Disagreement 4: Hard errors vs warnings on vocal-sync lead and variety rules.**
    27.1. *Strongest case for Fable's position:* Making `read-before-sung` (4 frames) and `entrance-variety` hard errors guarantees builders fix them before review (Fable 57.1, 57.2).
    27.2. *Strongest case for Astra's position:* Both 3 frames (backlog) and 4 frames (Fable ruling) are agent heuristics, not Jefferson's hard law (Brief 21; Astra Section 2, 6.4). Hard-blocking taste rules caused builders to game the validator before (Brief 62).
    27.3. *My position and deciding evidence:* Paper Trail landed words up to 26 frames early (Brief 66), which is an unmistakable bug, while a 5-frame lead on a dramatic slow turn can look fine. `engine/validate.ts:555` already supports error vs warning severity and targeted waivers.
    27.4. *Resolution:* Use three severity tiers in `KINETIC_V2`: hard errors for unambiguous technical failures (broken anchor, missing glyph, shadow ON, Suno paren visible, contrast <4.5, cap below format floor, crop, overlap, desync >6 frames early or >2 frames late); waivable errors for tight guidelines (4-frame early lead, <8-frame rest read, consecutive identical verse entrance); and non-blocking warnings/reports for global taste stats (>25% move share, rest-angle streaks, film-local verbs).

28. **Disagreement 5: Single engine-hosted runner (Fable) vs version-pinned film isolation (Astra, Neon).**
    28.1. *Strongest case for Fable's position:* Hosting `Root.tsx` and `node_modules` inside `Kinetic Type Engine/` reduces every video repo to pure data files with zero duplicated tooling (Fable 60).
    28.2. *Strongest case for Astra's and my position:* Running every video directly against one mutable checkout means opening an old video pinned to an earlier tag requires switching branches in the active engine repo, recreating the worktree mess (`Kinetic Type Engine HEAD check/`).
    28.3. *My position and deciding evidence:* Brief items 34 and 70 show both problems clearly: copied per-video `node_modules` + relative aliases broke `/tmp` worktrees, while unversioned shared checkouts made commit pins meaningless.
    28.4. *Resolution:* Keep the universal Remotion host and `node_modules` inside the engine, AND have the `kte` CLI resolve each film's pinned tag from an immutable local version cache (`~/.kte/releases/<tag>/`, created automatically from git tags outside the repo). Film folders stay 100% data-only while every historical video reproduces bit-for-bit at its exact tag.

29. **Disagreement 6: Perspective 2.5D camera via CSS 3D on outer `<svg>` (Fable) vs projected SVG geometry (Astra, Neon).**
    29.1. *Strongest case for Fable's position:* CSS 3D transforms on the root `<svg>` element are cheap to test with a 35-degree cap (Fable 54.2).
    29.2. *Strongest case for Astra's and my position:* CSS 3D warps a rasterized SVG bitmap, degrading sharpness on zoom and clashing with `SmearStage` sub-frame averaging (`engine/smear.tsx:18`) and viewport clipping. Meanwhile `engine/turn3d.tsx` already proves true SVG homography, and `TypeStage.tsx:61-63` shows the fragility of split render/validator transforms.
    29.3. *Resolution:* Defer perspective camera until after the core compiler, unified validator, and promoted transition library ship. Run a quick proof comparing CSS 3D root transform vs extending `turn3d.tsx`'s homography to the board plane; adopt whichever keeps sharp typography, smear compatibility, and exact validator projection parity.

## Part 6. Recommended architecture (one page)

122. **Modules (one repo, pure-type core, isolated sibling props).**
    - `core/`: immutable `EngineContext`, `FormatSpec` (`WIDE`, `VERTICAL_FULL`, `SHORT_916` with asymmetric `safeBox`), measured house metrics, layout, pure camera rig, SVG `TypeStage`, fixed `SmearStage`, `turn3d` projector, unified `WordFrameEval` (rendering + `visibleHull`).
    - `effects/`: typed, immutable `defineEffect` / `extendEffect` catalogue (separated `source`, `direction`, `pivot`, `timing`, `params`), `cascade` and `reverse` wrappers, Pulp Fiction paired recipes.
    - `score/`: `compileScore(ctx, show)` compiling declarative paired line cards into frozen `CompiledBoard[]`, indexed cues, unified seam plans, and responsive 9:16 restaging; `raw` escape hatch for custom set-pieces.
    - `songmap/`: Python/librosa + Node wrapper (`kte ingest`) for deterministic alignment cleaning, outlier clamping, section inference, stable line/word IDs, and `ear-check.md`.
    - `validator/`: single `KINETIC_V2` engine running on `WordFrameEval` + `visibleHull`, emitting tiered diagnostics, sync tables, and variety dashboards.
    - `host/` and `cli/`: engine-owned Remotion `Root.tsx` (reads `KTE_FILM`), `kte` CLI, section-cached parallel rendering, Node SVG golden-hash runner.
    - `../Kinetic Type Props/` (optional sibling package): `props/`, prop palette roles, object verbs, object panels, and prop validator plugin. Pure-type videos never import it.

123. **Per-line authoring format (`score/*.ts`).** One compact card per lyric line (or paired transition), addressed by stable ID and word text, compiling cleanly across 16:9 and 9:16:

```ts
export const verse1 = section('v1', [
  card('v1.4', {
    rows: ['NOW RAISE A GLASS TO', {text: 'ANT-MAN', size: 'hero'}, {text: 'mes amis', voice: 'adlib'}],
    source: landmark('v1.3', 'GLASS', {letter: 'last', part: 'foot'}),
    enter: {effect: 'turn', axis: 'y', pivot: 'left', stagger: 'word'},
    accents: [{word: 'ANT-MAN', effect: 'beatPunch', scale: 1.22}],
    camera: {move: 'quarter', dir: 'ccw', frame: 'hero'},
    fate: 'margin',
    leave: landmark('v1.4', 'ANT-MAN', {letter: 'T', part: 'stemTop'}),
    vertical: {camera: {move: 'step', frame: 'safe'}},
  }),
]);
```

124. **The new-video command and CLI.**
    - `kte new "<Title>" --audio <mp3> --alignment <json> [--palette pulp-ink] [--fonts house]` creates `~/Experiments/<Title> Music Video/` containing ONLY data: `film.json` (pinned engine tag, audio ref, edit lists), `song.json`, `analysis/song-map.v2.json`, `analysis/ear-check.md`, `look.ts`, `score/*.ts` (seeded from lyrics with `rows: 'auto'` and optional `--auto-draft` choreography), `renders/`, and `reviews/`. Zero `node_modules`, zero `Root.tsx`, zero `remotion.config.ts`, zero relative `../../` aliases.
    - Companion commands: `kte ingest <film>`, `kte check <film> [--format wide|vertical|short]`, `kte sheet <film>` (contact sheet), `kte render <film> [--section v1] [--format wide] [--draft]` (auto-incrementing versioned output filenames, section-cached, audio muxed), and `kte where <film> 1:23` (maps timestamped notes to exact card/row/effect).

125. **Defaults (encoding Jefferson's standing taste).**
    - `shadows: false` on all words and plates (if explicitly enabled per word, shadow offset scales as a fraction of cap height, never fixed 6 px).
    - `stripSunoParens: true` during `kte ingest` (sets `adlib: true` on stripped words; rendered via `look.voices.adlib`, never in brackets unless `keepParens: true`).
    - All sizing, margins, and framing derived from `format.safeBox` (`minCap` ~7.5% of safe short edge, WCAG contrast >= 4.5 against actual local ground/plate, `morphPin` defaults to `'screen'`, banned spellings auto-generated from `song.json` `display` map).

126. **Checks (`KINETIC_V2` + motion gate).**
    - *Tier 1 Hard Errors:* missing lyrics, broken anchors, unknown effect/param, missing font glyph, banned spelling, shadow ON under `shadows: false`, visible Suno parens, contrast <4.5, cap below format floor, safe-box crop during read window, `visibleHull` overlap, seam discontinuity, severe vocal desync (>6f early or >2f late without `preset`/`relight`).
    - *Tier 2 Guarded Errors (waivable with 1-line `why`):* tight vocal sync (>4f early), rest-read (<8f), consecutive identical entrance family on non-hook lines.
    - *Tier 3 Taste Dashboard (warnings/reports):* sync table sorted worst-first, per-section entrance-family share (>25% flagged), rest-angle streaks, `film:` local effects list.
    - *Regression & human gates:* Node SVG golden-hash checks on engine fixtures in CI; mandatory 480p `--draft` playback-with-sound review by the panel before Jefferson watches; master-first sign-off before Short/vertical cuts.

127. **Versioning and release hygiene.**
    - Engine releases are immutable semver git tags (`v1.0.0`, `v1.1.0`) with zero uncommitted files; `kte render --master` refuses a dirty engine tree and records the exact tag and commit in `film.json`.
    - `kte` executes films against an immutable local release cache (`~/.kte/releases/<tag>/`) checked out cleanly from git tags, so old videos always re-render identically without touching the active engine checkout.
    - Any `film:` local effect prototyped during a video is either promoted into `effects/` (with fixture + Effects Reel entry) and tagged before the next video starts, or deleted at wrap.

## Part 7. Expected score and what would still keep it below 10

128. **Projected score if Part 6 is built well: 9 / 10.** It would take a new pure-typography song from mp3 + alignment to a verified, synchronized, multi-format rough cut in minutes, shrink per-video code by roughly 80%, eliminate the recurring mechanical/taste rework loops (shadows, parens, sync, contrast, seams, framing), make 9:16 verticals genuine kinetic restagings instead of scrolling-feed rewrites, and give every video reproducible version pinning.

129. **What would still keep it below 10 / 10:**
    129.1. **Unproven in production across contrasting songs.** As Astra rightly argued, a 10 cannot be awarded on paper; it must be earned across at least two contrasting finished songs (such as a fast, dense hype track and a sparse, slower satire track) demonstrating <=2 owner revision rounds, >=80% scored lines without `raw` escapes, and zero manual seam patches.
    129.2. **Automatic layout and vertical reflow still have creative edge cases.** Automated safe-box wrapping and collision avoidance will reliably produce clean, legible layouts, but dense fast verses, tight counter nests, and aggressive typography jokes will still occasionally need hand-tuned `vertical` overrides or `raw` set-pieces.
    129.3. **SVG ceiling for true 3D spatial typography.** Even with projected `turn3d` strips and a Phase 3 2.5D perspective camera, heavy staggered 3D turns and camera orbits inside pure SVG hit DOM/rasterization and sharpness limits sooner than WebGL/Canvas2D.
    129.4. **Musical wit and comedic timing cannot be compiled.** Automation eliminates mechanical faults and repetitive habits, but knowing which word deserves a deadpan pause, a sudden scream, or a clever physical gag still depends on creative direction and Jefferson's judgment watching motion with sound.
    129.5. **Residual audio-aligner imperfection.** Suno's alignment tool will still occasionally misplace overlapped vocals, ad-libs, or rapid chants, leaving a small irreducible human ear-check step at the start of every song.
