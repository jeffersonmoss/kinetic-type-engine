# Pulp Fiction typography (Jarratt Moody): how the motion actually works

Studied by the main agent from three contact sheets at 4 frames per second (this folder; 20 s each, 0:00 to 0:58), 21 November 2026, after Jefferson asked whether we had really looked at it. Source video: `~/Experiments/Buy This IPO Music Video/reference/Pulp Fiction Typography - Jarratt Moody/`.

His words (21 November 2026): "I meant text rotating 90 degrees, rotating in from the Z axis to face the camera, spinning and diving." And: moves are crisp, "either clearly moving or not moving", never wobbling.

## What the video does
1. Scale is enormous. Hero words are bigger than the frame: single letters fill it (KE, IT, AT, N), and the camera tracks along a giant word letter by letter (WALLACE, LOOKS LIKE, GOD DAMN TIME). Small words beside them are tiny (one tenth the height or less). Mid-size type is rare.
2. Holds are dead still. "WHAT / T does Marsellus Wallace look like" holds about 4 s without a pixel of motion. Between holds the moves are fast and decisive, with heavy motion blur on the whip, then a dead stop.
3. The camera rolls the whole composition by 45 and 90 degrees. A giant N with a paragraph stacked beside it rolls until the paragraph runs diagonally, then vertically; layouts are built so the next line reads after the roll. Tilts are big and purposeful, never a few degrees.
4. Words are set at 90 degrees beside other words (WHAT COUNTRY running vertically beside YOU; TIME and BALD vertical beside BLACK; BRETT along a diagonal edge).
5. Words push words. AINT drops in on top of WHAT and shoves it down and off; NO lands on AINT; HEARD lands on EVER; FROM stacks above YOU. The new word enters and the old one is pushed out in one move.
6. Letters break apart and tumble away (LOOK LIKE scatters into loose falling letters, then the frame clears).
7. Anchor plus nest: one giant letter (T, N) holds the frame edge while a small block of text builds beside it line by line; then the anchor is revealed as part of the next word.
8. Crossings into depth: lines slide in along the slanted plane of the rolled composition; whole blocks swing in and out of the frame on the diagonal.
9. One typeface family (a heavy slab serif), with size, rotation and position doing all the work. Colour changes only with the scene ground (brown, then red).

## What our Room for One cut v2 did instead
Mid-size type, one phrase at a time on an empty frame, tilts of a few degrees, and a constantly drifting camera. Almost none of moves 1 and 3 to 7.
